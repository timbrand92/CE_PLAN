Source blocks, tanks and every other thing on the sheet

Every element works the same way

Three levels, exactly as for the pipe and the pit. Nothing is ever selected as "the whole network".

PART · a label, tag, letter, level text or arrow

Drags on its own. Stays where you put it. One click puts it back to the standard position.

ELEMENT · one pipe, one pit, one downpipe, one tank

Selects, moves, copies and erases as one thing, with its parts.

JOIN · a pipe end sitting on another element

Made by dropping the end on it. Broken by pulling it off. A loose end shows not joined

In every picture below: a grip, in the place it is today a join amber needs you

How each part moves

Every part has its own grip, where it is today. New: a part on a pipe slides along the pipe or pushes off it, cleanly, instead of drifting. A part you moved by hand keeps its new place when the pipe or pit later moves or changes, until you press Reset.

1 · Pipe label

Along · off · flip

Standard

2.5 mm, 0.75 mm clear of the pipe, near the middle.

Slide along (parallel)

Moves along the pipe. Stays the same distance off it.

Push off (perpendicular)

Moves away from the pipe. Stays at the same place along it.

Flip side

Jumps to the other side at the same distance. Always reads the right way up.

Reset puts it back at the standard distance and place.

2 · Invert level text

Along · off · flip

Standard

1.8 mm, red, at the pipe end, just clear of the pipe.

Slide along (parallel)

Moves along the pipe to clear a pit tag or a wall.

Push off (perpendicular)

Moves away from the pipe, square to it.

Flip side

Jumps to the other side of the pipe. The same at each end.

It still reads the invert of that end; only its place is yours.

3 · Flow arrow

Along · off · flip

Standard

7.5 mm long, beside the pipe.

Slide along (parallel)

Moves along the pipe.

Push off (perpendicular)

Moves away from the pipe, staying parallel to it.

Flip side

Jumps to the other side. It still points the same way.

Its direction is never set by hand. It always points downhill, read from the two inverts.

4 · Cover level text

Move · flip angle · flip corner

Standard

2.0 mm at 45 degrees, off the top-right corner.

Move

Drag it anywhere near the pit.

Flip the angle

Swaps between the +45 and the -45 position.

Flip the corner

Steps round the four corners of the pit.

It still reads the pit's cover level.

5 · Pit tag

Move · flip

Standard

3.5 mm in a circle, under the pit.

Move

Drag it anywhere near the pit.

Flip

Jumps to the opposite side of the pit.

Later, the pit moves

The tag keeps the place you gave it, measured from the pit.

The number inside is the pit's number; it is never typed into the tag.

6 · Inlet letters (DP, IO, FW, OF)

Drag · flip · flip

Standard

2.5 mm, beside the symbol.

Drag

Drag the letters to clear a wall or a pipe.

Flip left / right

As the block's flip grip does today.

Flip above / below

As the block's second flip grip does today.

The same for every inlet symbol.

7 · Tank note and WT tag

Move freely

Standard

Tag inside the symbol; note under it. (Third note line left off these small pictures.)

Move the note

Drag it anywhere.

Move the tag

Drag it out of the symbol if the tank is small.

Later, the tank changes

The note stays where you put it and reads the new litres.

The note keeps reading from the tank.

The rule for all seven

Your move is kept

  • A part you moved by handkeeps its new place when the pipe or pit later moves or changes
  • The gripsthe same ones as today, plus the clean "along" and "off" behaviour
  • Resetone click on the part puts it back to the office standard

3. Inlets (your source blocks)

Typed DP, IO, GIO, OF, FO, CDFW, as now. Each one is an element: the symbol and its letters. A pipe picked on one starts from it. Eight kinds.

How every inlet behaves

Pick it, and the pipe starts from it

1 · Type CD. Hover a downpipe.

The symbol under the cursor is outlined so you know the pipe will start there.

2 · Click it. Move away.

The pipe starts at the downpipe and follows the cursor at full width.

3 · Click a pit.

Joined at the downpipe and on the pit wall. Label and level text appear at the standard distances.

4 · The schedule already knows.

The pit's inlet row reads "from DP", with the pipe's size and invert. Nothing typed.

Counted, on one made-up two-unit job

Plain "DP" · your call

Both ends on nothing. It is drawn in full, with its label. Two "open end" lines on Checks.

One end on a pit

Joined at the pit, open at the other end. One line on Checks.

Situation 3  fine

Charged pipe, downpipe to tank only

A complete, correct pipe: joined at the downpipe and at the tank. No pit, so no schedule row. No grade in the label. Its check is the 300 minimum from gutter to tank inlet, not grade.

Situation 4  fine

Downpipe straight to a pit

A gravity pipe. The pit's schedule row reads "from DP".

Situation 5  fine

Downpipe joining a pipe part-way

A branch with no pit. The join sits on the pipe. An inspection opening can be placed at it; it is offered, not forced.

Situation 6  fine

Two pipes crossing

Not joined. Nothing is ever assumed from lines crossing; only an end dropped on something joins.

Situation 7  fine

Tank dropper and tank overflow

Each is a pipe from the tank edge to a pit. Each pit row reads "from WT1".

Situation 8  fine

Pipe to the discharge point

The end joins the council pipe, pit or kerb and carries the connection note. Amber only while the invert is assumed.

Situation 9  fine

A pipe with bends

One pipe, however many points. One label, on the straight you choose. Inspection openings sit at the bends.

Situation 10  fine

Twin detention pipes

Side by side between the same two pits. One label reading "2 x 225". Both count toward storage.

Situation 11  allowed, flagged

A pit with nothing joined

Allowed. It is scheduled with empty inlet and outlet cells and shows "no pipes" on Checks.

Situation 12  needs you

A second outlet on a pit

Allowed. Checks says "P2 has two outlets" and waits for the engineer: it may be right, or one may be an inlet.

Situation 13  needs you

A pipe drawn uphill

Allowed. The arrow follows the levels, not the way it was drawn. Checks says the far end is higher.

Situation 14  allowed, flagged

Levels not set yet

The label shows "AT 1 IN __" and the level texts "IL __". Nothing is guessed. One "levels not set" line on Checks.

Situation 15  fine

Existing council drain and pit

Shown in grey. Your pipe can join either. The plugin never moves or changes them.

Situation 16  fine

Line drains into pits

A trench grate or strip drain end joins a pit ("from TG"). An agricultural drain joins a silt pit ("from AGI").

Situation 17  fine

Copy a whole group

Two pits and the pipe between them, copied together: the copies keep their joins to each other, take the next numbers, and are not joined to the originals.

Choices I had to make

Where the design rules did not settle a case I drew the simplest thing that fits them. Each one is yours to settle.

For you to settle

22 · choices

  1. Inlets numbered or plain. This page draws plain "DP", "IO", "OF" as your sheets print now. Numbered (DP1, DP2) is possible; then the schedule would read "from DP3".
  2. Tank tag. I drew "WT1" and "WT2" because the criteria box and roof-to-tank box need to name each tank. Today the symbol reads plain "WT".
  3. Twin detention pipes. Drawn as one element that shows two bands and reads "2 x 225". The other way is two separate pipes side by side, as today.
  4. An inspection opening on a pipe. I have it sit on the pipe and ride with it, without cutting the pipe in two.
  5. Riser and dropper. Treated as ordinary pipes whose wording changes when an end sits on a tank, landing on the tank edge nearest the click.
  6. The word "APPROX" in the connection note. Kept while the invert is assumed. Whether it drops once the invert is confirmed is yours to say.
  7. Pit size. One fact. The schedule reads the size of the symbol, so they cannot differ (today they sometimes do).
  8. Typed names for things that have none in the hands today. Floor level, level tag, areas, discharge point, overland flow, read background: I wrote "press ..." only. You choose any typed names.
  9. "FO" and "GIO". The record says FO is a front overflow; I put it with Overflow. I used GIO for the grated inspection opening, as the block is named.
  10. Fall arrow grade. Read from two level tags when it is clicked on two; otherwise typed or left off.
  11. Erasing a tank. Its roof area stays and shows "no tank" in amber, rather than being erased with it.
  12. When things are built. I put side entry pit and kerb under Later (they come with outfall and site work); easement, tree zones, services and neighbour's footing checks under Release 2; council notes and legend under Release 1; drawing register under Later.
  13. Marking detention. A pipe or pit counts toward storage by a tick on that element, not by a separate kind.
  14. Legend. Full legend as now by default, with a choice to list only what the job uses.
  15. What an open end prints as. On screen I drew a small amber ring. I assumed it prints nothing extra (a plain pipe end) until you mark it capped, continuing off the sheet, or to kerb. What each of those three prints is yours to say.
  16. "Open end" and "not joined". I treat them as the same state: an end on nothing. One wording would be simpler; which one?
  17. A branch onto a pipe. A pipe end dropped part-way along another pipe joins there with no pit. An inspection opening is offered, not forced.
  18. Label on a pipe with bends. One label, on the straight you choose. More labels only if you add them.
  19. Copying a group. Pits and pipes copied together keep their joins to each other. Copied one at a time they come out unjoined.
  20. Flow arrow before levels are set. I have it point the way the pipe was drawn until both inverts exist; after that it follows the levels only.
  21. Two outlets, uphill pipes, pits with no pipes. All allowed and put on the Checks list. None is blocked.
  22. Cover level text corners. Flip steps it round the four corners of the pit and between the two 45 degree angles; it can also be dragged anywhere.
