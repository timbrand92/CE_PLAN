# Stage 1 digest: the rest of the August 2026 civil evidence notes

**Made:** Mon 16 Nov 2026 by a read-only helper under this chat, from the civil evidence folder (`C:\CE_DrawingIntelligence\02_Anonymised_Evidence\PILOT-C01_CIVIL`). **Coverage:** 68 notes found; 15 read in full; the drafting and engineering sections of all 30 per-job cards read; about 12 more skimmed; 9 not opened (the old command cards, progress log, handoffs and a few maps). **Status:** a digest of what the old notes say. "Observed" means the old study saw it on real jobs; it has not been re-checked here except where my own sheet reading agrees. Nothing in it is a decision.

## 1. Drafting grammar (observed unless marked)

1. **Sheet reading path:** warning notes across the top; plan upper-left / centre; pit schedule and detention criteria directly below or beside the downstream end; job details in a lower band; long section lower-centre when present; full legend and general notes in a right strip; title and revisions at the outer edge. (Agrees with the three sheets I read.)
2. **Text heights at 1:100 on A1:** pipe description 250, invert 180, pit cover 200, dwelling headings 350, plan title 700.
3. **Pipe label:** size and grade text parallel to the run, turned to stay readable. Measured about 139 off a 150 pipe in clear space, about 406 when crowded, about 592 off a 525 pipe. Old confirmed rule: keep the offset, centre along the run, shift only to clear another drawn item, and keep manual moves.
4. **Flow arrow:** a separate 750 leader parallel to the pipe; may sit on the other side from the text. Direction should come from levels, not the direction the line was drawn.
5. **Pits:** symbol, P-number tag, cover-level text and one invert text per connected pipe end are separate objects. No fixed side for the tag.
6. **Pit schedule:** loose text on a ruled block, not a table. Columns: pit, size, inlets (from / dia / invert), outlet (dia / invert), cover level, depth, lid and remarks. A pit with several inlets uses extra rows with the repeated cells left blank. Downpipes, overflows, floor wastes and paved pipes appear as "from" sources, not as pits.
7. **Detention criteria:** one text block listing permitted discharge, storage required, storage by member (pipe, each pit, each tank), total, top water level and orifice.
8. **Long section:** only 2 of the 30 main jobs had one, both for the outfall to the council pit. Rows and scales differed; no fixed row set recorded.
9. **Colours:** gravity blue, charged red, rising main green, agricultural drain black, architecture faded.
10. **Risk notes:** boxed, often red, placed at the spot they concern.
11. **Legend:** always the full office legend; a symbol in it does not mean the item is on the job.
12. **Set-up:** residential jobs attach one prepared background twice (ground and site, clipped, 50 m apart). Model space also holds parked template details that are not plotted.
13. **Sparse sheets are fine:** details are chosen for the job, not to fill space.

## 2. Beyond pits, pipes and tanks (what appears on the drawing)

1. **Pump station:** schedule row, pump specification box, wet-well section, green rising main to a receiving pit, then gravity to the kerb.
2. **Basement:** a separate basement plan; perimeter spoon / strip drains, floor wastes, agricultural drains, "drop to basement / from above" notes.
3. **Spoon drain:** a drawn strip with local fall arrows into pits or a floor waste.
4. **Trench / threshold grates:** line inlets at driveways and garage doors.
5. **Agricultural / subsoil drain:** a line along walls, joined through a 100 pipe or silt pit.
6. **Raingarden / buffer strip / permeable paving:** hatch plus section (filter layers, liner, underdrain, overflow pit), often with a catchment-area key.
7. **Outfall in the road or an easement:** narrow outfall plan, council standard details, long section, service-crossing clouds.
8. **Easement:** protected strip with clearance dimensions and "no pits in easement" notes.
9. **Industrial:** two or three A1 sheets; carpark slab and infill slab hatches, garden strips, a capacity check of an existing network instead of detention.
10. **Flood-affected site:** open undercroft, overland-flow arrows, ground level distinct from minimum floor level.
11. **Fill / earthworks:** cut-fill sheet or "fill to RL" zones.
12. **Soakage:** in the detail library and one spreadsheet; none of the 30 main jobs used it. (One of the three jobs I read did.)

## 3. Engineering practice (observed)

1. **Methods:** Boyd, OSD4W, rational flow, Manning pipe capacity, orifice formula, pump and wet-well sizing, charged-system check, STORM for treatment.
2. **Inputs:** permitted discharge and required storage (council or permit), areas, runoff coefficients, the existing council drain level (usually "confirm on site").
3. **Worked out:** storage by member, orifice diameter and head, top water level, pipe capacity and velocity, pump duty, wet-well volume.
4. **Storage is a sum:** detention pipes + pits + the detention share of tanks + the wet-well share. Reuse volume is never counted.
5. **One tank, several duties:** e.g. 5,000 litres as 4,000 reuse + 1,000 detention.
6. **Two orifices:** a small one at the tank (30 to 40) and the final one at the control pit (50 to 86); usually only the final one was calculated.
7. **Charged pipes carry roof water only;** pumping is limited to basements, ramps and agricultural drains.
8. **Disagreements found on real jobs:** drawing total against calculation by 0.01 to 0.02 cubic metres; orifice 55 against 56, and 40 against 38 or 39; tank splits differing between plan and detail on the same stamped sheet; rising main 50 against 80 and 4 m against 50 m; pump pit size differing; a schedule with two rows both "P1"; the standard quoted with three different years.
9. **Copied workbooks:** job folders hold template packs carrying other jobs' numbers; of 564 workbooks checked, none matched a master.
10. **Central spreadsheets:** orifice coefficient 0.6 in general against 0.8 in one old sheet; a rainfall workbook that was broken; storage worked from nominal, not internal, pipe diameter.

## 4. Standard detail library (observed)

1. **Families:** orifice / control pits; water tanks and pump stations; charged-system diagram; pipe bedding; pit and junction-pit details; service penetrations and trench beside footing; spoon drains; kerbs, pavements and joints; raingardens, swales, buffer strips; soakage; roof details; council note blocks (27 for one council); long-section examples; legend and disclaimer.
2. **What varies:** council version, manufacturer and model, above or below ground, tank size and split, orifice size, plate height, levels, pipe sizes, pit number, lid class, spoon-drain width and grade.
3. **Caution:** masters are mixed with old job examples carrying real job values.

## 5. Council differences that change what is drawn

1. **Method** differs (Boyd, OSD4W, council-supplied figures, a permit rate per hectare, a fixed imperviousness baseline). Old confirmed rule: go by the job's own discharge advice and permit, never by suburb.
2. **One council:** all detention underground, its own standard orifice pit, no credit for above-ground tanks.
3. **Others:** approval limited to detention only; groundwater limits on basements; checklists and mark-up colours; pits kept out of easements; as-constructed certificates; kerb outlets by gravity only; arrows proving surface fall away from neighbours.

## 7. What the old notes themselves say went wrong

1. **Bringing in an outside engineer's plan:** lost the control pit and the council connection, made 47 false pits out of labels, joined one pipe to itself, guessed 100 sizes, and still reported no errors. It could also wipe the previous attempt before the user answered No.
2. **Guessing meaning:** pipe type changed by nearness, layer or colour; one "type" trying to say status, position and purpose at once.
3. **Workarounds:** tanks and agricultural drains stored as pits.
4. **Too many pieces:** 99 commands, 18 buttons on one panel, about 133 settings in one place, background behaviour hard to predict.
5. **Detail picker too coarse:** put in generic details whenever pipes existed.
6. **Parked template blanks read as live design.**
7. **Proposed but never built:** one linked item with movable parts; a read-only "check current drawing" first; plain drafting words.
