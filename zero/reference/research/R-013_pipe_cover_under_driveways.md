# R-013 — Pipe cover under residential driveways (Victoria)

## Checked at source, 19 Nov 2026

Checked against the office's own copies: AS/NZS 3500.3:2018 and AS/NZS 3500.3:2003 (office PDFs), the office's AS 2566.1-1998 (scanned, could not be read), and council guideline documents in the office Councils folder. Cover means finished surface to top of pipe unless marked "below underside of pavement".

### Plain answer

1. **The standard supports 450 mm only where the driveway has no pavement** (gravel, unsealed, grass). For a plastic pipe under a reinforced concrete driveway the standard's floor is 100 mm below the underside of the slab; under brick or unreinforced concrete paving for light vehicles it is 75 mm below the underside.
2. **The standard does not treat a detention or storage pipe differently.** The 2018 OSD clause (7.10) has no cover rule; it sends below-ground OSD to AS 2865 (confined spaces) only. Large-diameter pipe cover is left to the pipe standards (clause 6.2.5 (a) to (f)) and the maker.
3. **Councils mostly ask 300 mm under a driveway, and several say so for detention pipes in words.** Banyule, Bayside 2026, Manningham, Yarra (2026 and earlier), Maroondah and Monash give 300 mm. Frankston 250 mm, Casey 100 mm below the pavement. Only Yarra 2026 (one note) and Bayside's own table give 450 mm for this case.
4. **So the 300 mm driveway preset is the figure the council documents support. 0.45 m is a conservative office choice**, backed by the standard only for an unpaved driveway. Watch how "300 mm" is measured: Manningham measures it to the underside of the driveway, not the finished surface.
5. **How sure:** high for the standard (table read in the PDF's own text order, both editions); medium-high for councils (text read directly, but some tables came out jumbled, noted below).

### AS/NZS 3500.3:2018, clause 6.2.5 and Table 6.2.5 (office copy, page 69 to 70)

Two columns: "ductile iron, galvanized steel" and "plastics" (PVC, PE, PP). Figures in mm.

1. **Not subject to vehicles, no pavement, single dwelling:** iron/steel 100, plastics 100.
2. **Not subject to vehicles, no pavement, other than single dwelling (covers dual-occupancy and unit sites):** iron/steel 100, plastics 300.
3. **Not subject to vehicles, brick or unreinforced concrete pavement:** iron/steel 100, plastics 100, both below the underside of the pavement.
4. **Subject to vehicles, other than roads, no pavement:** iron/steel 300, plastics 450.
5. **Subject to vehicles, reinforced concrete pavement for heavy vehicles:** iron/steel nil, plastics 100, below the underside of the pavement.
6. **Subject to vehicles, brick or unreinforced concrete pavement for light vehicles:** iron/steel nil, plastics 75, below the underside of the pavement.
7. **Roads, sealed:** iron/steel 600, plastics 600.
8. **Roads, unsealed:** iron/steel 600, plastics 750.
9. **Construction equipment loading or embankment:** iron/steel 600, plastics 750.
10. **Agricultural land:** iron/steel 600, plastics 600.
11. **Way out of the table (clause 6.2.5):** cover may instead follow AS/NZS 2041 (corrugated metal), AS/NZS 2032 (PVC), AS/NZS 2566.2 (flexible pipe), AS/NZS 3725 (concrete and FRC), AS 4060 (clay) or AS/NZS 2033 (PE).
12. **Under buildings (clause 6.3.6):** at least 25 mm between top of pipe and underside of a reinforced concrete slab, plus protection from damage.
13. **Appendix K worked example (Melbourne villa units):** within the property 100 mm except under the driveway; under the paved driveway 75 mm below the underside of the pavement; the commentary says roughly 100 mm below a reinforced concrete pavement, "say 200 mm overall".
14. **Detention pipes:** clause 7.10 (OSD) covers overflow, orifice plates, access, sumps, ladders over 1.2 m deep, and AS 2865. No cover figure, no special rule for storage pipes.

### AS/NZS 3500.3:2003, clause 7.2.6 and Table 7.1 (office copy, page 72)

Columns: "cast iron, ductile iron, galvanized steel" and "other authorized products" (includes PVC).

1. **Not subject to vehicles, no pavement, single dwelling:** iron nil, other 100.
2. **Not subject to vehicles, no pavement, other than single dwelling:** iron nil, other 300.
3. **Not subject to vehicles, brick or unreinforced concrete pavement:** iron nil, other 50.
4. **Subject to vehicles, no pavement:** iron 300, other 450.
5. **Reinforced concrete pavement, heavy vehicles:** iron nil, other 100.
6. **Brick or unreinforced concrete pavement, light vehicles:** iron nil, other 75.
7. **Roads, sealed or unsealed; construction or embankment loading:** iron 300, other 500.
8. **Footnotes:** pavement values are below the underside of the pavement; one footnote ties values to compliance with the pipe standards; another refers to a 50 mm overlay. The symbols linking footnotes to cells did not survive text extraction, so which cell each applies to is not certain.

### What changed 2003 to 2018 (driveway-relevant)

1. **Unchanged:** plastics 450 (no pavement, vehicles), 100 (reinforced concrete), 75 (light paving), 300 and 100 (untrafficked).
2. **Changed:** untrafficked brick or unreinforced paving rose from 50 to 100 below underside; roads rose from 500 to 600 (sealed) and 750 (unsealed); iron/steel columns went from nil to 100 for untrafficked ground.

Researched 19 Nov 2026 (Melbourne). Public web only. No job data used.

## ANSWER FIRST

1. **Both office rulings can be defended, for different surfaces.** 450 mm is the AS/NZS 3500.3 figure for plastic pipe where vehicles cross ground with no pavement. 300 mm is not the standard's figure for a trafficked driveway; it is its figure for untrafficked ground away from a single dwelling's garden (and a figure some councils use as a general minimum).
2. **A concrete driveway slab changes the answer a lot.** Under a reinforced concrete vehicle pavement the standard's plastic-pipe figure drops to 100 mm, measured below the underside of the slab (2003 edition, opened at source).
3. **A detention pipe is not treated differently by AS/NZS 3500.3.** The table goes by pipe material and surface, not by whether the pipe stores water. Large-diameter storage pipes are governed in practice by the maker's structural design for the pipe class and load. I found no maker guide I could open, so this part is not confirmed.
4. **No council I could open says 450 mm for a detention pipe.** Casey's guideline uses a 450 mm diameter pipe as its example, which is a pipe size and not a cover.

## 1. AS/NZS 3500.3 (stormwater drainage)

1. **2003 edition (with Amendments 1 and 2, reissued 2010), clause 7.2.6 and Table 7.1.** Opened at source, but through a scanned full-text copy with poor character recognition, so the table layout is probable, not certain. Clause 7.2.6 says cover is not less than Table 7.1, and allows cover set by the pipe standards instead (including AS/NZS 2566.1 for flexible pipe). Columns are "cast iron, ductile iron, galvanised steel" and "other authorised products" (this includes PVC-U). Plastic-pipe values:
   - **Not subject to vehicles, no pavement, single dwelling:** 100 mm.
   - **Not subject to vehicles, no pavement, other than single dwelling:** 300 mm.
   - **Not subject to vehicles, brick or unreinforced concrete pavement:** 50 mm.
   - **Subject to vehicles, other than roads, no pavement (unsealed):** 450 mm (iron/steel column: 300 mm).
   - **Subject to vehicles, other than roads, reinforced concrete pavement, heavy loading:** 100 mm.
   - **Subject to vehicles, other than roads, brick or unreinforced concrete pavement, light loading:** 75 mm.
   - **Roads, sealed or unsealed; construction equipment or embankment loading:** 500 mm.
2. **How it is measured.** The pavement-type values (50, 75, 100 mm) are below the underside of the pavement, not from the finished surface. The standard's Appendix K worked example says "a minimum of 100 mm below the pavement (say 200 mm overall)" for a Melbourne villa development, and 75 mm below the underside for a paved driveway. Opened at source.
3. **2025 edition, Table 6.2.5.** Not checked at source. I saw it only through an online calculator that paraphrases it. It gave the same plastic-pipe pattern (100 / 300 / 100 / 450 / 100 below a reinforced concrete vehicle pavement) but 600 mm under a sealed road, where the 2003 table says 500 mm. That may be an edition change; I could not confirm it. The standards store lists 2025 as the current edition (published 17 Apr 2025).
4. **2015 and 2018 editions.** Could not open. Both PDFs I found were unreadable.

## 2. Other national sources

1. **NCC 2022 Housing Provisions, clause 3.3.5(3)** (Australian Building Codes Board). Opened at source. Deemed-to-satisfy for 90 mm Class 6 uPVC drains only: 100 mm under soil, 50 mm under paved or concrete areas, 75 mm under reinforced concrete with light vehicles, 100 mm under paving with light vehicles. This is a small house-drain rule, not a storage pipe rule. NCC 2016 Volume Two clause 3.1.2.5 (opened by a helper) gives the same figures.
2. **AS/NZS 2566.1 and 2566.2 (buried flexible pipe).** Not opened. All I can say, from the 3500.3 clause above, is that 3500.3 lets cover be set by structural design under 2566.1 instead of the table.
3. **AS/NZS 3500.2 (sanitary), 2021.** Not checked at source. A New Zealand drainlayer study-guide site (a secondary source I rate as low reliability) gives 300 mm gardens, 450 mm paved residential driveways, 600 mm unsealed driveways. These do not match the 3500.3 table. Do not rely on them.
4. **Pipe makers' web pages.** Form Direct Environmental, "stormwater pipes" page (not checked at source against the standard): 300 mm untrafficked, 450 mm trafficked not roadway, 600 mm sealed road, and "subject to variation by the asset owner". A drainage blog says trafficable PVC 450 to 600 mm and concrete or ductile iron can reduce to 300 mm (not checked at source; the standard's extract is not shown).
5. **Maker installation guides for PVC (Iplex, Vinidex) and for large polypropylene or concrete detention pipe (Humes, Rocla, Hynds, Atlantis, Reece).** I could not open any of them (files came back unreadable). No maker figure is reported.

## 4. Which source supports which ruling

1. **0.45 m for a detention pipe under a driveway** is supported by AS/NZS 3500.3 (2003 Table 7.1, and the 2025 table by secondary summary) for plastic pipe under a vehicle-loaded surface with no pavement. It is also the figure for sealed concrete pavers or unsealed gravel in practice. No council or maker source I opened says it for detention pipes specifically.
2. **300 mm as a general driveway preset** is supported by Knox (summary only) as a general minimum, and by 3500.3 only for untrafficked ground away from a single dwelling, and for iron or steel pipe under vehicles on unsealed ground. For plastic pipe under a trafficked driveway it is below the standard's unpaved figure.
3. **Under a reinforced concrete slab**, the standard's floor is 100 mm below the slab underside (Casey agrees at 100 mm). So 300 mm or 450 mm are both conservative against the standard there. Whether a big-diameter detention pipe needs more under a slab is a maker structural question I could not check.

## RECOMMENDATION (a preset the engineer can change)

1. **Preset 450 mm to top of pipe** for any pipe, ordinary or detention, under a driveway or other vehicle-loaded ground, finished surface to top of pipe. It matches the standard's plastic-pipe figure for the unpaved case and the stricter office ruling.
2. **Offer a "reinforced concrete driveway slab" choice** that lowers the minimum to 100 mm below the slab underside for ordinary pipe (3500.3 and Casey). Show it as the standard's floor, not the default.
3. **For a large detention pipe, warn the drafter to check the maker's installation guide and the council** whatever the choice, because the standard's table does not cover large-diameter storage pipe structure.
4. **Keep the figure typed by the engineer, with the source shown beside it.**
