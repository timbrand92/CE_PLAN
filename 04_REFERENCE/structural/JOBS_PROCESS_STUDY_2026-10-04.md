# How a CE structural job runs: a study of six recent jobs (4 Oct 2026)

**What this is:** a read-only study of six recent structural jobs from the office jobs folder, asked for by Tim on 4 Oct
2026. **It is a reading of the files, not a Tim ruling.** Tim to correct.

**How it was done (DATA-01):** folder and file names, file dates and the text of the newest issued structural PDF in each
job. No drawing (DWG) was opened. Nothing was copied into any repository. Cloud-only files were skipped, so nothing was
downloaded. Jobs are called Job A to Job F. The real folder names are in a local-only file outside every repository.

**The six jobs:**
1. **Job A (2026):** small. External slab and stairs. 4 sheets. Footing plan and details only.
2. **Job B (2026):** two-storey multi-unit development on a raft. 14 sheets. Steel and timber framing. Many engineer markups.
3. **Job C (2026):** house with a timber subfloor and steel framing. 8 sheets. Large A1 title block.
4. **Job D (2026):** multi-level house with a basement, band beams and block walls. One DWG per sheet. Still at preliminary.
5. **Job E (2025):** basement retention, suspended slabs, screw piles. 15 sheets. About 15 months from set-up to issue.
6. **Job F (2025):** two-storey multi-unit on a raft. 12 sheets. Five construction revisions.

**Also used, for the sheet order only:** the drawing registers of 17 more structural sets from 2025 and 2026.

**How to read the marks:** "seen in N of 6" counts the six jobs. "One job only" means exactly that. "Not seen" means I
looked and did not find it. "I do not know" means the files do not say.

## 1. How a job runs, from first email to issue

### Documents in

1. **Architectural documents for the quote** arrive first, in a dated folder such as "Docs for Quote". Seen in 6 of 6.
2. **A fee proposal** follows in Admin. Seen in 6 of 6. Job D had four fee variations before the work started (one job only).
3. **Architectural CAD files** arrive later, in a dated folder ("CADs", "Updated CADs"). Seen in 6 of 6.
   Updated architecturals keep arriving during the job. Job B had three architectural rounds (one job only for that count).
4. **The soil (geotechnical) report** lands in Engineering\Soil Report. Seen in 6 of 6.
   It drives the site class, founding depth and bearing in the Geotechnical Note.
5. **Site photos or test pit records** sometimes arrive. Seen in 2 of 6 (A, D).
6. **Builder's comments and RFIs** arrive mid-job. Seen in 2 of 6 (C builder's comments, F RFIs).

### Who does what

1. **The engineer** designs. Evidence: the "SD" (schematic design) prints and markups, the analysis files in
   Engineering\Comps, and the "DESIGN" initials in the title block. Seen in 6 of 6.
2. **The drafter** sets up, drafts, plots and issues. Evidence: the "DRAWN" initials, the set-up PDFs and the plot logs.
   Seen in 6 of 6.
3. **A checker / approver** signs the title block ("CHECKED", "APPROVED"). Seen in 6 of 6. In the classic title block the
   checker and approver were the same person in 3 of 3 jobs that use it. In the A1 title block the "APPROVED" box was left as "-" in 3 of 3.
4. **Markups come from more than one person:** two different people's initials or names on markup folders in 4 of 6 (B, C, D, E). I do not know their roles.

### Documents out, in order

1. **Set-up PDF ("STR set up"):** the architectural plans brought into the CE sheets, before any design. Seen in 6 of 6.
   Job E had three set-ups (plans, basement, elevations). One job only.
2. **Engineer's schematic design ("SD"):** framing and footing plans marked up by the engineer, sometimes with a member
   schedule. Seen in 5 of 6 (B, C, D, E, F). Job A had one sheet "for discussion" instead.
3. **Preliminary issues P1, P2, P3...:** title block says "PRELIMINARY ISSUE" and "NOT TO BE USED FOR PRICING PURPOSES".
   Seen in 6 of 6.
4. **Tender issues T1, T2, T3:** seen in 2 of 6 (B, E).
5. **Check plot:** a full print checked by the engineer just before a revision. Seen in 3 of 6 (C, D, E).
6. **Construction issue Rev A, then B, C...:** title block says "CONSTRUCTION ISSUE". Seen in 5 of 6 (D is still at P2).
7. **A design certificate document ("Reg 126", one per construction revision) in Admin.** Seen in 4 of 6 (A, B, C, F).
   Not seen in D (not at construction yet) or in my listing of E. I think this is the Victorian design compliance certificate. I have not verified it.
8. **Civil drawings run alongside** on most jobs, in the same issue folders ("STR+CVL REV A"). Seen in 4 of 6.

### Rhythm (from file dates)

1. **Architectural CADs to set-up PDF:** 2 to 5 days. Seen in 4 of 6 (A, B, C, D). Longer in E and F.
2. **Set-up to the engineer's SD:** about 3 to 4 weeks. Seen in 3 of 6 (B 23 days, C 24 days, F 24 days).
3. **SD to P1:** 1 to 9 days. Seen in 3 of 6 (B 9 days, C 13 days, F 1 day).
4. **Markup to the next revision:** usually 1 to 4 days, up to a week. Job B: 3, 3, 4, 1 and 7 days. Job C: 1 day after the
   check plot markup. Job D: 1 to 7 days. Seen in 3 of 6.
5. **Number of issues:** Job A 5, Job B 9, Job C 4 plus a check plot, Job F 7, Job E about 20 structural issue folders.
6. **Revisions after construction issue are common:** A went to Rev C, F to Rev E, E to Rev C. Seen in 4 of 6.
   Triggers seen: updated architecturals (B, F), site photos and pipe locations (A), coordination (E).
7. **Typical small job:** about 2 weeks from documents to construction issue (A). **Typical double-storey:** about
   3 to 6 months, mostly waiting for the engineer and the architect (B, C, F). **Basement:** over a year (E).

### Folder structure and file naming

1. **Every job uses the office folder template:** Admin, Architectural, Certification, Civil, Electronic Issue,
   Engineering (Comps, Markups, Soil Report), Memorandums, RBS Checklist, RFI's, Superseded, Xref. Seen in 6 of 6.
2. **Incoming folders are dated:** "YYYY-MM-DD_what it is". Seen in 6 of 6.
3. **Issue folders are dated with the revision:** "YYYY-MM-DD_STR_REV A". Seen in 6 of 6.
4. **The live structural DWG sits in the job root,** named job, "STR", site, revision. Older revisions move to
   Superseded. Seen in 4 of 6 (A, B, C, F).
5. **One DWG per sheet** ("-S01.dwg", "-S02.dwg"): seen in 1 of 6 (D), and in 2 more 2026 jobs outside the six.
   The office template folder also holds a basement sheet set with one DWG per sheet.
6. **Architectural plans come in as Xrefs** in the Xref folder. Seen in 6 of 6. One combined Xref file in 4 of 6 (A, B, C, F); one Xref per level (Basement, Ground, First, Roof, Schedules) in 2 of 6 (D, E).
7. **Markups are in dated sub-folders** named after the revision they are for ("mark ups for REV T1"). Seen in 4 of 6
   (B, C, D, E). Some markups are marked-up DWGs, not PDFs (C, E).
8. **A plot log** sits in the issue folder. Seen in 4 of 6. The one I read (A) shows each layout S01 to S04 plotted to PDF
   on an A3 full-bleed sheet at 1:1, about one second per sheet.
9. **A schedule export file ("TIM SCHEDULE.csv")** sits in Engineering. Seen in 2 of 6 (A, B). I do not know what writes it.

## 2. The structural drawing set, in the order a drafter produces it (roof down)

**Sheet order on paper is bottom-up** (notes, footings, floors up, roof, bracing). Seen in 6 of 6 and in all 17 extra
registers. This matches Tim's UX-SHEETS-01 ruling. **Drafting order is roof down** (Tim, UX-STRUC-13). The files do not
show the drafting order directly. One job outside the six issued its upper framing first as a preliminary, which fits
roof down. One more issued "roof and ground" together first.

**The standard set (seen in 13 of the 17 extra registers, and in B and F):** S01 General Notes sheet 1, S02 General Notes sheet 2,
S03 Ground Floor Footing Plan, S04 to S06 Footing Details, then Remedial Works Details, First Floor Framing Plan,
First Floor Framing Details sheets 1 to 5, Roof Framing Plan, Bracing Details. Small and odd jobs merge sheets
(A, C, D, E).

Below, in roof-down drafting order:

### 2.1 Roof framing plan

1. **Scale 1:100.** Seen in 6 of 6.
2. **Tags:** roof beams RB, roof lintels RL, rafters R, girder trusses RT, columns C, double studs DS, LVL and MGP10 sizes in
   the schedule. Seen in 6 of 6. Tag families match the 26 Sep framing study (`FRAMING_PLAN_PRACTICE_2026-09-26.md`).
3. **Roof truss note:** trusses and their bracing to the manufacturer's design. Seen in 6 of 6.
4. **Member schedule:** "ROOF MEMBER SCHEDULE" or "ROOF FRAMING MEMBER SCHEDULE", MARK and SIZE. Seen in 2 of 6 (C, E).
5. **Typical count:** about 10 to 40 tags on the plan (text count, rough).

### 2.2 Upper floor framing plan(s)

1. **Scale 1:100.** Seen in 6 of 6 where an upper floor exists (5 of 6; A has none).
2. **Tags:** floor beams FB1 to FB15, lintels L1 to L6, columns C, PC, SC, double and triple studs DS, TS, outriggers OR,
   pergola beams PB, glulam GL, floor joists FJ, bracing truss BT, wind beam WB (Job B). Modifiers CANT, CONT, UNDER, (O).
3. **Bracing:** Type A (strap) and Type B (ply) wall bracing symbols in the legend. Seen in 5 of 6.
4. **Legend:** "DENOTES ..." lines for bracing types, loadbearing walls under, double studs, set-downs, bathtubs, outline of
   the floor over. Seen in 6 of 6.
5. **Numbered framing notes 1 to 5:** AS 1684, bracing and tie-downs, galvanised steel, joist bracing, connectors. Seen in
   6 of 6.
6. **First floor member schedule:** MARK and SIZE, grouped "Structural Supports", "First Floor Members", "Common Members".
   It sits on the framing plan or its details sheet. Seen in 4 of 6 (B, C, D, F).
7. **Typical count (Job B):** about 15 floor beams, 6 lintels, about 10 column and stud types.
8. **Suspended concrete floors** (D, E): band beams BB in an "RC BEAM SCHEDULE", RC columns in an "RC COLUMN SCHEDULE",
   additional reinforcement plans. Seen in 2 of 6.

### 2.3 Ground floor: slab, raft or subfloor

1. **Raft slab on ground** with edge beams EB, internal beams IB and thickenings TH: seen in 3 of 6 (B, F, and D's slab).
2. **Timber subfloor** (stumps, bearers, joists, subfloor notes): one job only (C).
3. **Basement retention and suspended ground slab:** seen in 2 of 6 (D, E). Soldier pile retaining wall schedule (D, E),
   screw pile schedule with safe working loads (E only).
4. **Legend bottom-left:** raft slab note (thickness, mesh, cover, f'c, bar chairs, membrane, sand) and symbol keys (steps,
   set-downs, saw cuts, steel columns, stud walls). Seen in 6 of 6. Matches the 26 Sep raft study.
5. **Footing schedule sideways:** one column per mark (EB1, EB2, EB3, IB1, IB2, TH1 to TH4), rows for type, width, depth,
   reinforcement. Seen clearly in Job F; the words "FOOTING SCHEDULE" appear in 6 of 6.
6. **Set-down and step notes:** "builder to verify locations of all steps, set-downs and rebates". Seen in 5 of 6.
7. **Saw cuts:** seen in 4 of 6.

### 2.4 Footing plan (last)

1. **On most houses the footing plan and the raft slab plan are one sheet,** "Ground Floor Footing Plan" (S03). Seen in
   6 of 6 in some form.
2. **Bored piers** under point loads and on poor ground. Seen in 6 of 6 (in notes or on plan).
3. **Strip and pad footings** SF and PF. Seen in 3 of 6 on plan.
4. **Footing details** follow on 1 to 3 sheets at 1:20 (some 1:10). Seen in 6 of 6.
5. **Remedial works details** sheet: seen in 2 of 6 (B, F) and in 9 of the 17 extra registers.

### 2.5 Notes and schedules on every set

1. **General notes S01 (and S02):** G1 to G11 general, design loads, concrete C1 to C19 with grades and cover table,
   foundations F1 to F12, slab on ground SG1 to SG10, site works W1 to W5, concrete symbols legend, steelwork, masonry,
   stud wall framing. Seen in 6 of 6.
2. **Geotechnical Note:** site class, the soil report reference and date, founding depths and bearing. Seen in 6 of 6.
3. **Drawing register on S01:** sheet number, revision, title. Seen in 6 of 6.
4. **Masonry lintel schedule and windowsill trimmer schedule** in the notes. Seen in 6 of 6 (office boilerplate).
5. **Scales:** plans 1:100, details 1:20, some details 1:10. Seen in 6 of 6 (1:10 in 3 of 6).

### 2.6 Title block and revisions

1. **Classic A3 title block:** project, client, design, drawn, checked, approved, issue status, issue date, job reference,
   drawing number, one revision letter, "SCALE AS SHOWN", "PRINT IN FULL COLOUR". No revision history. Seen in 3 of 6
   (A, B, F).
2. **A1 horizontal title block, plotted reduced to A3** ("Reduced plot - A1 to A3 - 2 x Scale"), with a revision table
   (number, description, drawn, checked, approved, date) and a scale bar. Seen in 3 of 6 (C, D, E). The office template
   folder holds both.
3. **Revision letters:** P = preliminary, T = tender, A, B, C = construction. Seen in 6 of 6.
4. **A status that did not follow the revision:** Job A's Rev C sheets still say "PRELIMINARY ISSUE" and "NOT TO BE USED
   FOR PRICING". One job only. I do not know if that was meant.

## 3. The repeated manual work (the boring end)

How long each takes is mostly not recorded. Where file dates show it, I say so.

1. **Set-up:** bring the architectural plans in as Xrefs, sheet them, plot a set-up PDF. 2 to 5 days after the CADs arrive
   (4 of 6), but that is waiting time, not working time. Working time: I do not know.
2. **Tag numbering:** FB1, FB2... L1, L2... EB1, IB1, TH1. Renumbering after design changes. Every set (6 of 6). Time: I do
   not know.
3. **Member and footing schedules:** typed or built to match the tags (MARK and SIZE; footing sideways). 6 of 6.
   Time: I do not know.
4. **Legends and notes:** office boilerplate, edited per job (site class, soil report date, slab note). 6 of 6.
5. **Geotechnical Note:** copied from the soil report by hand (report number, date, class, depths, bearing). 6 of 6.
6. **Drawing register on S01:** sheet list and revisions kept by hand. 6 of 6.
7. **Title block per revision:** revision letter, status, date, initials, revision table row. 6 of 6. One job showed a
   status left behind (A).
8. **Plot to PDF and issue folder:** a dated folder per issue, PDF of every layout, plot log. Seen in 6 of 6. Plotting itself
   took about one second a sheet in the log I read.
9. **Move the old DWG to Superseded and rename the live DWG to the new revision.** 4 of 6.
10. **Design certificate per construction revision** (Word file to PDF in Admin). 4 of 6.
11. **Re-issue after markups:** 1 to 4 days typical from markup to new revision (3 of 6 with clear dates). The drafting
    in that window is the markup work plus all of items 2 to 9 again.

## 4. Where decisions are made, and the handover points

### Decisions (engineer)

1. Member sizes, spans, steel and timber choices: in the SD markups and the Comps. 6 of 6.
2. Load assumptions, wind class, bracing: in the general notes and the Comps. 6 of 6.
3. Footing type and size, pier depth, slab thickness and mesh: in the SD footing markup and the soil report. 6 of 6.
4. Basement retention and suspended slab design: D, E.

### Mechanical (drafter)

1. Set-up, Xrefs, layouts, title blocks.
2. Drawing what the markup says, tagging, numbering, schedules, legends, notes.
3. Plotting, issue folders, superseding, the register.

### Handover points

1. **Architect to CE:** CADs and PDFs into Architectural (dated).
2. **Drafter to engineer:** the set-up PDF. 6 of 6.
3. **Engineer to drafter:** the SD or markup PDF (dated folder). 5 of 6.
4. **Drafter to engineer:** P1 or a check plot. 6 of 6.
5. **Engineer to drafter:** markups for the next revision. 4 of 6.
6. **CE to client / builder / certifier:** the issue folder PDF, plus the certificate at construction. 6 of 6.

## 5. What I could NOT learn without opening the DWGs

For a later headless read-only study (layers, blocks, tags) on COPIES, to be scheduled by the desk:

1. Layer names and what goes on each layer.
2. Text heights and text styles per scale; dimension styles.
3. Tag offsets from members (how far, which side, rotation).
4. Block names used for columns, piers, bracing symbols, tags and schedules.
5. Whether tags and schedules are CE plugin objects, plain text, tables or blocks.
6. How many layouts per DWG, viewport scales, and the A1 versus A3 set-up in paper space.
7. Xref structure per level and how the levels line up (needed for the roof-down load trace).
8. Title block attribute names and how revisions are entered.
9. Colours, line weights and the plot style table used.
10. The real drafting order (which level was drawn first). File dates inside a DWG do not show it. I do not know if anything
    does.
11. Why some sets show 1:80 or 1:40 scale text (B, D). I do not know.
