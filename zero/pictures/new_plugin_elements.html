How each part moves

Every part has its own grip, where it is today. New: a part on a pipe slides along the pipe or pushes off it, cleanly, instead of drifting. A part you moved by hand keeps its new place when the pipe or pit later moves or changes, until you press Reset.

1 · Pipe label

Along · off · flip

Standard

2.5 mm, 0.75 mm clear of the pipe, near the middle.

Slide along (parallel)

Moves along the pipe. Stays the same distance off it.

Push off (perpendicular)

Moves away from the pipe. Stays at the same place along it.

Flip side

Jumps to the other side at the same distance. Always reads the right way up.

Reset puts it back at the standard distance and place.

2 · Invert level text

Along · off · flip

Standard

1.8 mm, red, at the pipe end, just clear of the pipe.

Slide along (parallel)

Moves along the pipe to clear a pit tag or a wall.

Push off (perpendicular)

Moves away from the pipe, square to it.

Flip side

Jumps to the other side of the pipe. The same at each end.

It still reads the invert of that end; only its place is yours.

3 · Flow arrow

Along · off · flip

Standard

7.5 mm long, beside the pipe.

Slide along (parallel)

Moves along the pipe.

Push off (perpendicular)

Moves away from the pipe, staying parallel to it.

Flip side

Jumps to the other side. It still points the same way.

Its direction is never set by hand. It always points downhill, to the lower of the two inverts.

4 · Cover level text

Move · flip angle · flip corner

Standard

2.0 mm at 45 degrees, off the top-right corner.

Move

Drag it anywhere near the pit.

Flip the angle

Swaps between the +45 and the -45 position.

Flip the corner

Steps round the four corners of the pit.

It still reads the pit's cover level.

5 · Pit tag

Move · flip

Standard

3.5 mm in a circle, under the pit.

Move

Drag it anywhere near the pit.

Flip

Jumps to the opposite side of the pit.

Later, the pit moves

The tag keeps the place you gave it, measured from the pit.

The number inside is the pit's number; it is never typed into the tag.

6 · Inlet letters (DP, IO, FW, OF)

Drag · flip · flip

Standard

2.5 mm, beside the symbol.

Drag

Drag the letters to clear a wall or a pipe.

Flip left / right

As the block's flip grip does today.

Flip above / below

As the block's second flip grip does today.

The same for every inlet symbol.

7 · Tank note and WT tag

Move freely

Standard

Tag inside the symbol; note under it. (Third note line left off these small pictures.)

Move the note

Drag it anywhere. The leader follows and still points at the tank.

Move the tag

Drag it out of the symbol if the tank is small.

Later, the tank changes

The note stays where you put it and reads the new litres. The leader re-aims at the tank.

The note keeps reading from the tank. Its leader arrowhead always stays on the tank.

8 · On an angled pipe, and round a bend

Along follows the pipe · off is square to it

Pipe at an angle: along

"Along" follows the pipe, whatever its angle. The label turns with it.

Pipe at an angle: off

"Off" is always square to the pipe, not straight up the screen.

Round a bend

Slid past a bend, the label carries on along the next straight and turns to suit.

One grip does both

Drag mostly along the pipe and it slides. Drag mostly away and it pushes off.

One grip for both moves is my choice. Two separate grips is the other way: see the choices at the end.

9 · When the pipe changes under a moved part

It stays on the pipe

You slid the label toward one end

It is now well off the middle, where you want it.

Later the pipe is shortened past it

The label slides back onto the pipe and keeps its distance off. It is never left hanging in space.

Mirror

Parts go with the element. Text is never left reading backwards.

Rotate

Parts turn with the element. Text still reads the right way up.

Mirror and rotate are AutoCAD's own commands; the parts simply go with their element.

3. Inlets (your source blocks)

Typed DP, IO, GIO, OF, FO, CDFW, as now. Each one is an element: the symbol and its letters. A pipe picked on one starts from it. Eight kinds: six take water in or give access, two (grated overflow, surcharge pipe) let it out when the system is full.

How every inlet behaves

Pick it, and the pipe starts from it

1 · Type CD. Move the cursor over a downpipe.

The symbol under the cursor is outlined so you know the pipe will start there.

2 · Click it. Move away.

The pipe starts at the downpipe and follows the cursor at full width.

3 · Click a pit.

Joined at the downpipe and on the pit wall. Label and level text appear at the standard distances.

4 · The schedule already knows.

P2's second inlet row reads "DP", with the pipe's size and invert. Nothing typed.

The pipe end dropped on the kerb line joins there and takes the kerb wording for its note. It is a legal point of discharge like any other.

Choices I had to make

Where the design rules did not settle a case I drew the simplest thing that fits them. Each one is yours to settle.

For you to settle

26 · choices

  1. Inlets numbered or plain. This page draws plain "DP", "IO", "OF" as your sheets print now. Numbered (DP1, DP2) is possible; then the schedule would read "from DP3".
  2. Tank tag. Tanks are drawn tagged "WT", as today, and named "water tank 1" and "water tank 2" in the boxes, as your sheets do. Tagging them WT1 / WT2 on the plan is possible if you want it.
  3. Twin detention pipes. Drawn as one element that shows two bands and reads "2 x 225". The other way is two separate pipes side by side, as today.
  4. An inspection opening on a pipe. I have it sit on the pipe and ride with it, without cutting the pipe in two.
  5. Riser and dropper. Treated as ordinary pipes whose wording changes when an end sits on a tank, landing on the tank edge nearest the click.
  6. The word "APPROX" in the connection note. Kept while the invert is assumed. Whether it drops once the invert is confirmed is yours to say.
  7. Pit size. One fact. The schedule reads the size of the symbol, so they cannot differ (today they sometimes do).
  8. Typed names for things that have none in the hands today. Floor level, level tag, areas, legal point of discharge, overland flow, read background: I wrote "press ..." only. You choose any typed names.
  9. "FO" and "GIO". The record says FO is a front overflow; I put it with Overflow. I used GIO for the grated inspection opening, as the block is named.
  10. Fall arrow grade. Read from two level tags when it is clicked on two; otherwise typed or left off.
  11. Erasing a tank. Its roof area stays and shows "no tank" in amber, rather than being erased with it.
  12. When things are built. I put side entry pit and kerb under Later (they come with outfall and site work); easement, tree zones, services and neighbour's footing checks under Release 2; council notes and legend under Release 1; drawing register under Later.
  13. Marking detention. A pipe or pit counts toward storage by a tick on that element, not by a separate kind.
  14. Legend. Full legend as now to start with, and a choice to list only what the job uses.
  15. What an open end prints as. On screen I drew a small amber ring. I assumed it prints nothing extra (a plain pipe end) until you mark it capped, continuing off the sheet, or to kerb. What each of those three prints is yours to say.
  16. "Open end" and "not joined". I treat them as the same thing: an end on nothing. One wording would be simpler; which one?
  17. A branch onto a pipe. A pipe end dropped part-way along another pipe joins there with no pit. An inspection opening is offered, not forced.
  18. Label on a pipe with bends. One label, on the straight you choose. More labels only if you add them.
  19. Copying a group. Pits and pipes copied together keep their joins to each other. Copied one at a time they come out unjoined.
  20. Flow arrow before levels are set. I have it point the way the pipe was drawn until both inverts exist; after that it follows the levels only.
  21. Two outlets, uphill pipes, pits with no pipes. All allowed and put on the Checks list. None is blocked.
  22. One grip or two for along and off. I drew one grip: drag mostly along the pipe and the part slides; drag mostly away and it pushes off. Two separate grips is the other way.
  23. Moving a pit without its pipes. One held key while dragging. Which key is yours to say.
  24. A pit dropped on a pipe. The pipe splits into two pipes joined to the pit, and waits for you to set the pit inverts. The other way is to leave the pipe whole and flag it.
  25. Which pit line a pipe end stops on. Every pit is drawn with both lines: the inner pit wall (solid) and the outer dashed line round it. I drew pipe ends stopping at the inner wall, because today's drawings show pipes stopping about 175 from the centre of a 350 pit. Stopping at the outer dashed line is the other way.
  26. Cover level text corners. Flip steps it round the four corners of the pit and between the two 45 degree angles; it can also be dragged anywhere.
