# Current rulings: The job and the office (how the office works, the Job window, the Job Manager)

Current rulings as of 18 Nov 2026, 5:30 pm (Melbourne), from decisions D-001 to D-297. If this page and DECISIONS.md disagree, DECISIONS.md wins and this page is stale.

## What is decided now

### Facts Tim gave about practice

1. **Council stormwater work is mostly single houses and units,** under half a day each; one person on small jobs, mark-up and drafter on units; the office applies to council for the discharge point; sheets are layout plan, notes and details, computations; long sections only when council asks; rework comes from architect changes, council sending it back and the drawing not matching; design often starts on assumptions and there is no set way of showing what was assumed (D-030).
2. **Design and drafting meet either way, per job:** small jobs straight in the drawing by one person, bigger jobs by mark-up and a drafter; the plug-in suits both (D-025).
3. **2 to 5 people will use the civil tools in the first year** (D-026).
4. **A "civil site job" apart from the stormwater and pavement sheet exists "sometimes"** (D-029).
5. **Site and impervious areas are measured by a mix, job by job** (D-020).
6. **The office designs while it draws,** and pipes are often drawn before they belong to a run (D-175).
7. **A pit's cover level does not come from the survey,** which is often pre-demolition; the engineer sets it from the internal house levels and the surrounding new and old pavement levels (D-178).
8. **Drafters start a new job from the office template** (D-191).
9. **Some laptops are used for drafting** (D-193).
10. **Plans are drawn at 1:1 and the paper layout scales them to 1:100** (D-194); **sometimes the drawing's units are metres** (D-217).
11. **Staff have muscle memory for how things look and feel now;** the design must align with it (D-032).
12. **The pit schedule wording on issued sheets** is as recorded in D-113, with one row per inlet (D-113).
13. **PIT BLOCK 3 is Tim's own newer pit block,** using stretching instead of many visibility states (D-215).
14. **Stage 1, how the office designs and draws civil jobs: "Approved as good enough to build on";** Tim corrects it whenever he sees something wrong (D-117).

### The Job window

15. **One Job window, typed once per job,** is change 4 of the five input changes (D-171). **Contents added 18 Nov:** the site's rainfall figures with their source (D-249) and the slab thickness per kind of paving (D-293).
16. **New jobs first:** old hand-drawn drawings still open and plot; a tool to adopt them comes later (D-027); job facts found in a copied drawing are treated as assumed until confirmed (D-191).

### The Job Manager

17. **Job tracking belongs in the office's Job Manager program;** the plug-in hands it what only the drawing knows and shows it back in the Manager window (D-071; see MANAGER).

### Added 18 Nov 2026, D-230 to D-297

18. **How a site's rainfall figures reach the office (D-249):** "A mix, depends on council". Some councils state the intensities to use, otherwise the Bureau's table. The Job window takes either: figures given by the council (held with the council, source recorded) or the Bureau table pasted or typed for the site; each figure marked with where it came from.
19. **Slab thickness is set in the Job window, per kind of paving (D-293):** "should read from the plans and then you can set it in each jobs"; no office preset; typed over on a pipe where it differs; until it is set, cover under paving reads "Not known" (see LEVELS_AND_STANDARDS item 24).
