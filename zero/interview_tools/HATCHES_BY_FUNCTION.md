## The answer first

1. **The office hatches by pen-colour layer, and one layer does several jobs.** The layer number is the pen colour (HATCH 21, HATCH 74 and so on); the legend on the sheet says what it means on that drawing. Tim's example is confirmed in the evidence. HATCH 74 SOLID has legends:
   - "DENOTES OUTLINE OF PROPOSED FIRST FLOOR OVER" (most often, in old jobs and in the master);
   - "DENOTES LOCATION AND EXTENT OF BAND BEAMS" (in the master drawing, and once in the old jobs);
   - "RAINWATER GARDEN" / "RAINGARDEN" (civil).
2. **The most used hatch layers in the old jobs:**
   - **HATCH 21:** 16 of 21 jobs; concrete, slabs, footings, blinding.
   - **HATCH 201:** 15; existing to be retained.
   - **HATCH 51:** 14; wet area set-downs.
   - **HATCH 141:** 13; roof truss outline.
   - **HATCH 74:** 13; first floor over, band beams, rain garden.
   - **HATCH EARTH:** 10; earth in sections.
   - **HATCH 11:** 9; concrete and earth in sections.
   - **WALL HATCH:** 9; brick and block.
3. **The same layer carries different patterns.** For example, HATCH 21 is mostly AR-CONC (sections) and SOLID (plan slabs), with a few EARTH and DOTS. So the office list needs "what it does", "layer" and "pattern" as three separate things. One layer can be offered for several functions.
4. **So the proposal for OFFICEHATCH / OH** is a list sorted by what the hatch does (band beam, first floor over, wet area set-down, existing to be retained...). Each line names its layer and pattern, and the window shows the function words first. Several functions may share one layer, as HATCH 74 does today. Tim decides the list.

## How this was found

1. **Old jobs:** 218 drawings (DXF) from 21 old jobs in the local evidence store were read as text, read only. Every hatch's layer and pattern was counted. For small hatches (legend samples), the words written just to the right were read as its legend.
2. **Master drawing:** the same legend reading on the office master drawing copy.
3. **Private details:** jobs are counted only, never named. No file name, address or client words are written here; legend texts with an address shape were skipped.
4. **Limits:**
   - **Legend matching is by position:** it sometimes catches a neighbouring label instead of the true legend. Single hits are weak; repeated hits are strong.
   - **Architects' drawings are included:** their layers (A-...) were read too, but are left out of the lists below.
   - **Function counts can mislead:** the job count against a function is how many jobs use that layer and pattern at all, not how many used it for that function.

## Scale (Tim: "make sure you look at the scale as well")

The pattern scale is the number that sets how big the pattern is drawn. Below: the scales old jobs used most (with how many hatches), against the master drawing's samples. Solid hatches have no scale.

1. **Same in old jobs and the master (safe to fix in the list):**
   - **HATCH 11 - LOAD BEARING WALL OVER, ANSI31:** 50 (112 hatches).
   - **HATCH 231 - LOAD BEARING WALL UNDER, DOTS:** 20 (93).
   - **HATCH 47, ANSI31:** 60 (21).
   - **HATCH 6, DOTS:** 80 (16); 60 a few times.
   - **HATCH 150, ANSI31:** 50 (13).
   - **HATCH 253, ANSI37:** 100 (7).
2. **Different, and this matters:**
   - **HATCH 21 concrete in section (AR-CONC):** old jobs mostly use 3 (552 hatches), then 5 (407), 4 (277) and 2.5 (273). The master samples use 1 to 2. This is probably Tim's "wrong scale" (TA-003): taken from the master at 1 to 2, it comes out two to three times too fine.
   - **HATCH 11 concrete (AR-CONC):** old jobs 4 (141), 2 (69), 2.5 and 5. It is now retired to HATCH 21 (TA-052).
   - **HATCH EARTH, EARTH:** old jobs 40 by far (1,757), then 35 and 30. The master has 8, 35 and 40.
   - **WALL HATCH brick and block (JIS_LC_8A):** old jobs 110 (121), then 100. The master has 19.8, 22, 100 and 110.
   - **HATCH TIMBER, WOODFACE:** old jobs 1000 (77), then 800, 654.5 and 640. The master has 200 and 654.5.
   - **HATCH STUD WALL, WOODFACE:** old jobs 800 (41), then 1000. The master has 160 and 800.
   - **HATCH SAND, DOTS:** 40 and 50.
   - **Plain HATCH 231, DOTS:** 10 (181) and 20 (134).
3. **Why one hatch shows several scales:** most likely the same hatch drawn for different sheet scales, because plans are drawn full size with a 1:100 viewport (D-194) and sections and details sit in 1:20 or 1:10 viewports. Which viewport each hatch sat in was NOT read, so this is not proven.
4. **Proposal for OFFICEHATCH / OH:**
   - **Scale in the list:** each line holds its scale for a 1:100 plan, and its scale for a 1:20 detail where it is used there.
   - **Window:** the tool picks the scale from the viewport or the drawing's sheet scale, so "wrong scale" cannot happen.
   - **Starting figures:** the most-used scale in old jobs, not the master's sample, for Tim to confirm.
