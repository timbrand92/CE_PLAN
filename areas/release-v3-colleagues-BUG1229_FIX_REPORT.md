# BUG-1229 fix report: New Pipe worked-out downstream level is never flatter than the grade chosen (Thu 29 Oct 2026, about 11:15 Melbourne)

## ANSWER FIRST

**FOR TIM**

1. **The fault is found and a fix is built.** Draft PR **#1838** on the newest Civil line (3.0.85, branch `claude/rel-20261026z`). Nothing installed, nothing merged, nothing opened in AutoCAD. BUG-1229 stays OPEN.
2. **What went wrong, in drawing terms:** on your 9.2 m pipe at 1 in 100, the window worked the fall out as 92 mm, then rounded the downstream LEVEL to the nearest 10 mm. 19.358 became 19.36, so the real fall was only 90 mm, flatter than 1 in 100. Then, as you kept typing the upstream level, the window mistook its own rounded answer for a fall you had set, worked the grade back from it (9.2 / 0.09 = 102) and wrote 102 into your "Set my own" box.
3. **What changes for a drafter:** the grade you choose is now a minimum. Type 19.45 on that pipe and the window gives **19.35** (a 100 mm fall, 1 in 92), the grade box stays **100**. A 20 m pipe at 1 in 100 from 19.45 gives **19.25**. A level you type is never changed. Levels you type on both ends are held exactly as before.
4. **The rule I picked, and why:** two rules were possible. (a) Keep your chosen grade and round the level DOWN (steeper). (b) Round to the nearest 10 mm and show the real grade it ends up at. I built (a), because you said the grade you pick is a minimum and 1 in 102 is "shallower than what it should be". It costs at most 10 mm of extra fall.
5. **The switch:** `pipe.suggestedLevelNeverFlatterThanGrade`. It ships ON. Set it to false in the settings file to get the old answer back (19.36 and 1 in 102). It is in the settings file only, no row on the Settings page.
6. **What the step is:** the 10 mm comes from your existing Settings "Calculated decimals" (2 = 10 mm). Nothing is hard-coded; no new setting was needed.
7. **My own mistakes first:** (a) I started an empty Python window by mistake and it wrote a 64 MB junk file; I found it, checked no Python was left running, and deleted that one file. (b) My first background launch of the test run died silently when my shell closed, so it cost about 10 minutes before I noticed; I relaunched it properly. (c) 14 of my own first tests failed: they started from CD's stock levels 1.10 and 1.20, which count as "you typed a downstream", and one expected number of mine (19.455) was wrong. Product code was right; I fixed my tests.
8. **Robot case to add:** `N32_cd_grade_never_flatter`, with a switch-off control `N32_off` (details at the end). It is not written.
9. **Status:** built and unit-tested only. Plan figures for this one fix: built yes, unit-tested yes, tested in AutoCAD no.
