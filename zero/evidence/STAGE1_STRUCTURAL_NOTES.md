# Stage 1 notes: what a structural set is made of

**Made:** Mon 16 Nov 2026. **Source:** the issued PDFs of the two structural copies (JOB_D, two units, construction issue, 14 sheets; JOB_E, two townhouses, preliminary issue, 11 sheets), every sheet read as a picture at whole-sheet size. Both are two-storey timber-framed dwellings on a raft slab with some steel. **Not seen:** any single-storey house, alteration, waffle slab, warehouse, basement or pool job; the drawings' insides (layers, blocks); close-up reading of small text. **Status:** my reading, not yet confirmed by Tim. No job details recorded.

## The sheet set (A3-size sheets, same order on both jobs)

1. **S01 General notes 1.** Job title in large text, design loads box (floor, balcony, roof, garage live loads; wind region, terrain, wind speed), general, site works, concrete notes with two boxes (concrete grade by element; cover by element), foundations, slab on ground, the drawing register, and a coloured **geotechnical note** box (site class, soil report number and date, founding material and bearing capacity for each footing kind).
2. **S02 General notes 2.** Masonry, steelwork, timber notes; stud wall framing box (stud, plate, nogging, jamb sizes by wall height band); double studs, laminated members, temporary bracing, columns; trench-beside-footing and tree root barrier details.
3. **S03 Ground floor footing / slab plan.** The plan, its legend, the footing schedule (on the plan sheet on one job, on the next sheet on the other) and a small member schedule for ground-level timber.
4. **S04 to S06 Footing details.** Typical sections for each footing type, slab steps, set-downs, re-entrant corners, mesh laps, column base plates (with a base plate schedule), services through footings, and job-specific sections cut on the plan (boundary beside a neighbour's footing; block wall on a bored pier beside an easement).
5. **S07 First floor framing plan.** The plan and its legend, numbered notes, one or two job-specific sections.
6. **S08 First floor member schedule and joist details.** The schedule (mark and size) plus typical joist-to-beam details and job-specific steel connection details.
7. **S09 Connection details.** A full sheet of typical timber-to-timber, timber-to-steel, steel-to-steel and tie-down details with two small schedules (joist hanger by member depth; cleat plate and bolts by steel beam size).
8. **S10 to S12 spare detail sheets.** Present in the template; blank when not needed.
9. **S13 Roof framing plan.** The plan, legend, roof member schedule, roof truss note, typical truss and top plate details.
10. **S14 Bracing details.** Strap bracing, ply bracing (with capacity in kN and a ply thickness box), and how bracing walls fix to trusses, joists and external walls.

Every sheet carries the same title strip: logo, project, client, drawing title, design / drawn / checked / approved initials, issue stamp box, issue date, reference number, drawing number, revision.

## Elements on the plans

**Footing / slab plan**
1. Slab areas by hatch (raft slab, suspended raft slab, paving slab), each defined in the legend with thickness, mesh, cover, concrete strength, membrane and sand.
2. Slab beams as dashed lines with a type mark at each run: EB edge beam, IB internal beam, numbered by size (EB1, EB2, IB1, IB2).
3. Slab thickenings (TH1, TH2, TH3) as dashed squares under posts and columns.
4. Strip footings (SF), pad footings (PF), bored piers (BP), stumps (ST).
5. Floor levels as text (FFL or FSL with a level); slab steps with a step symbol; wet area set-downs by hatch.
6. Small coloured squares for what lands on the slab: steel columns (red, tagged C1(O) for "over"), double and triple studs (DS, TS), and a hatch strip for internal load-bearing walls over.
7. Blinding concrete zones by hatch; retaining walls (sleeper walls SRW1 to SRW3 with excavation sequence numbers; block walls BRW1).
8. Section marks pointing to the detail sheets; leader notes for local changes ("locally widen slab beams and provide 2 layers of mesh").
9. Background from the architect in grey; title boundaries with bearings and distances; trees with protection circles; easements; survey levels.

**Floor framing plan**
1. The floor over shown by colour fill; roof areas outside it by a second fill.
2. Beams as heavy lines with a mark along them: FB floor beam, LRB lower roof beam, WB wind beam, TR trimmer, GL garage lintel; suffixes CANT. (cantilevered), CONT. (continuous), LOWER. On the townhouse job marks carry the dwelling number in front (1FB1, 2FB1).
3. Lintels over openings (L1 to L8) on the wall line.
4. Joists as a span arrow with a mark (FJ1); rafters likewise (R1, R2); roof trusses (RT).
5. Steel columns (C1, C2), stub columns (SC1), portal columns (PC1, PC2), small coloured squares for double / triple studs under beam ends.
6. Bracing walls drawn as coloured dashed runs along walls with a letter in a circle: A strap brace (red), B ply brace (blue), S1 on the roof plan.
7. Load-bearing walls under shown by a hatch strip.
8. Numbered notes pointing at places (NOTE 1, NOTE 2) with the wording below the plan.

**Roof framing plan**
1. Truss area by colour fill with truss direction lines; girder trusses (GT, TGT) as heavy chain lines.
2. Roof lintels (RL1 to RL5), roof beams (RB1 to RB6), rafters (R1), posts (P1).
3. Bracing walls of the upper storey, hip and ridge lines.

## Schedules (mark and size, typed)

1. **Footing schedule**: one column per footing type; rows for width, depth, reinforcement. 9 to 11 types per job.
2. **Member schedule**: mark and size, grouped under headings (common members, lintels, floor beams, lower roof beams, roof lintels, roof beams). Sizes are text such as "2 No 290 x 45 F17 KDHW", "200 UB 22 or 150 UC 30", "300 deep posistruts at 450 max. cts. to manufacturer's design", sometimes with a back-span and cantilever limit. About 35 to 45 marks per job.
3. **Sleeper retaining wall schedule**, **column base plate schedule**, **joist hanger** and **cleat plate** schedules.
4. **Drawing register** on S01.

## How things tie together

1. **Mark to schedule.** Every beam, lintel, footing and column on a plan carries a mark; its size lives only in the schedule. The same mark can appear many times on a plan (IB1 appears dozens of times).
2. **Type to detail.** Each footing type has a typical section that says "refer schedule" for its sizes; job-specific sections are cut on the plan and numbered with the sheet they came from.
3. **Level to level.** What sits on a level is shown on the level below: columns and studs "over" on the slab plan, load-bearing walls "over" and "under" by hatch, thickenings under posts. This is the load path, and it must agree between the roof, first floor and slab plans.
4. **Soil report to footings.** The geotechnical note sets founding depth and bearing for each footing kind and is quoted by the details.
5. **Architect to structure.** All plans sit over the architect's background; many notes send the builder to the architect's drawings for set-downs, fire ratings and levels.

## Fixed frame versus moving parts

1. **Same on both jobs:** both general notes sheets almost word for word, the legends, the typical details (most of S04, S05, S09, S14), the stud framing box, the bracing types, the title strip.
2. **Changes with the job:** the loads box values and geotechnical note, the plans themselves, the marks and their sizes, which footing types exist, job-specific sections and steel connections, retaining walls, numbered plan notes, how many detail sheets are used.

## Things seen that went wrong by hand (observed on the issued sheets)

1. **Register out of step with the set.** On the 2024 job the drawing register lists 11 sheets with the roof plan as S10 and bracing as S11; the issued set has 14 sheets, S10 to S12 are blank spare sheets, and the roof plan and bracing are S13 and S14. On the 2025 job the register lists all 14 with the unused three greyed out, and the blank sheets are left out of the PDF.
2. **Schedule heading copied and not changed.** On the townhouse job the member schedule has two groups both headed "Townhouse 1 - Floor Beams"; the second holds the 2FB marks.
3. **Blank sheets issued** with a full title strip on the 2024 job.

## First observations for planning (mine, to be tested with Tim)

1. Structural has the same shape as civil: a **fixed frame** (notes, legends, typical details), a **model drawn once** (members with marks, footings with types, what bears on what), and **tables that repeat it** (schedules, register).
2. The structural "type once" is the **mark**: a beam's size is typed in the schedule and the mark is placed on the plan by hand; nothing checks that every mark on a plan has a schedule row, or that every row is used.
3. The **load path between levels** is drawn by hand three times (roof, first floor, slab). This looks like the structural twin of the civil levels problem.
4. Where the sizes come from (calculation, span tables, software, engineer's mark-up) is **not visible on the sheets**. To ask Tim.
5. Tim drafts **roof down**; the sheet set is numbered footings up. To ask Tim how the two orders meet in practice.
