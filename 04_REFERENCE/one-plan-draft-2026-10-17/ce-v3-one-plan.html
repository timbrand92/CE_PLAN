CE Version 3: how it feels to draw a job with it

This page is about the experience: what a drafter clicks and sees, and how every CE tool fits together so a job gets drawn faster, more consistently and with fewer mistakes. It is built from the answers you have already given on how CE should look and behave. Delivery dates are at the bottom.

The idea in one sentence

You draw the real things once (pits, pipes, beams, footings), and CE keeps everything that depends on them in step for you: labels, levels, schedules, notes, the OSD block, checks and title blocks. You never type the same number twice, and the drawing always matches the design.

Six things a drafter needs to understand, and that's all

1 · JobSet it up once

Job number, address, council, rating report, and markup or from scratch. Every title block, note and check reads from here. Change the council and the notes and checks follow.

2 · ObjectsThings that know each other

A pit knows its pipes. A pipe knows its two pits and its grade. A beam knows the post under it, and that post knows the footing below. Move a pit and its pipes follow, with a preview.

3 · Live outputText you never retype

Pipe labels, IL text, pit labels, member tags, the Pit Schedule, member schedules and the OSD block all update themselves. Hand-typed text is kept and only flagged.

4 · SettingsEvery number in one place

Minimum cover, text heights, layers, council values. Each is marked council, job or office, with where it came from. CE warns you; it never blocks you.

5 · ChecksOne list watching the drawing

It stays quiet when all is well ("Network OK, 24 pipes"). Click a problem to zoom to it. Safe fixes are pre-ticked and apply with one Undo.

6 · SheetsFrom drawing to PDF set

New sheet with title block and viewport at office scale. Issue picks the sheets that changed, the next revision letter and the dated folder.

The front door: one CE tab, laid out in job order

Left to right is the order a job runs. Gold buttons are the five or so daily tools in each panel; everything else sits in a drop-down or is typed. The middle panels switch between Civil and Structural depending on the drawing. Select a pipe, pit or beam and its own tab appears, as in AutoCAD. Proposed layout

  1. Header: the CE mark, a one-line summary of what you are editing, and its status at top right.
  2. One page: no scrolling and no hidden tabs. Tabs appear only for genuinely separate topics.
  3. Live preview: the drawing shows the change as you type, in a preview colour until you Apply.
  4. Footer: extra tools at the bottom left; Cancel and the gold Apply at the bottom right. Enter applies and Escape cancels.
  5. Same window for new and edit: New Pipe and Edit Pipe look identical, and so will New Beam and Edit Beam.
  6. It opens where you left it and remembers your last sizes the next day.

How it feels, minute to minute

Placing keeps going.

Click Pit and place P1, P2, P3. The window opens for each and it carries on until Enter. Pipes snap to a pit within 75 mm and never guess between two.

The cursor tells you.

Size, grade and length show beside the cursor while you draw, and turn the warning colour the moment cover or grade fails.

Change once, everything follows.

Lower P3 by 100 mm. The pipes regrade in the preview, and the labels, IL text, Pit Schedule and OSD block update. One Undo puts it all back.

AutoCAD's own commands still work.

Erase a pit and its pipes are left with free ends, and CE says so. Copy makes real CE copies, renumbered. Trim, Extend, Mirror and Rotate keep the text readable.

Everything is one click away.

Double-click or use Edit to get the window, Ctrl+1 for values, right-click for a short CE group. Select a pipe and the Pipe tab appears.

Finding things.

Type P7 or B12 in the CE find box to zoom to it. Click any schedule or Checks row to zoom, then Back. The arrow keys walk you along the network.

Quiet unless it matters.

One bell collects the minor messages. Only a failure, a colleague holding the drawing or an update ready pops up. The CE panel shows one health line.

Unknown stays Unknown.

No survey level? It says "Unknown" or "Assumed", never a made-up 0.00. Your typed values are always kept.

Your protections are kept.

CE never unlocks a layer, edits inside an xref or saves your drawing for you.

It learns your office.

Where pit labels sit and where tags go are learnt from your past drawings. Every suggestion says why, and never changes an engineering rule.

Where the speed and quality come from

  1. Nothing typed twice. The job, levels, sizes and areas are entered once, and every label, schedule and block reads them.
  2. Nothing out of step. Change the drawing and the text follows inside the same Undo, so the plan, schedule and calculations always agree.
  3. Looks like ours. Every schedule block, label, symbol, layer and linetype matches your existing drawings, and a robot checks it against real past jobs.
  4. Mistakes caught early. One Checks list watches cover, grade, council rules, unsupported posts and missing legend items while you work.
  5. Fewer clicks. Placing keeps going, Enter repeats with your last sizes, and the five daily tools in each panel are the big buttons.
  6. Fast and solid. Every command has a speed limit, and anything over a second shows progress with Cancel. Big drawings, locked layers and xrefs are all tested.

Where today's plugin does not match this yet

  1. Structural feels like a different program. It has its own tab of about 313 commands sorted by object type, older-style windows and its own settings file.
  2. Checking has seven doors that give different counts.
  3. Most new tools are switched off, so the "everything follows" feel is not visible yet.
  4. Only 4 Civil windows have the New Pipe look, and Edit Pipe does not look like New Pipe yet.
  5. The Pit Schedule waits when you type a level, instead of changing the pit.
  6. No one-click issue in either discipline yet.
