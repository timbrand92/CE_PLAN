Redrawn after your correction of 16 Nov. Mock-ups, with realistic made-up numbers. Not approved. How it is built inside AutoCAD is still to be proven in the research stage; I mark where.

Your question: one pipe and two pits that work together, not one glued object?

Yes. Connected, not merged.

A pipe is one element. A pit is one element. Each carries its own parts. They are joined at the pit wall, and the join can be made or broken like snapping a line to a point. Nothing ever selects "the whole network".

1. Three levels, so it never gets sticky

  • Part · a label, an arrow, a level text, a tagdrags on its own; remembers where you put it
  • Element · one pipe, or one pit, with its partsselects, moves, copies and erases as one thing
  • Join · a pipe end sitting on a pit wallmade by dropping the end on a pit; broken by pulling it off

What each ordinary AutoCAD action does

  • Click a pipethat pipe only. The pits are not selected.
  • Move a pitthe pipe ends joined to it follow. Their far ends stay put.
  • Move a pipe on its ownit comes off its pits. Each loose end shows not joined
  • Drag a pipe end onto another pitit joins there, on the wall.
  • Copy a pit or a pipea plain new one with the next number. Not joined to anything.
  • Erase a pitits pipes stay. Their ends show not joined
  • Stretch, mirror, rotate, undoas AutoCAD always does.
  • Move a pit without its pipeshold one key while dragging (which key: your call).

Nothing here is automatic design. A loose end is a warning on the Checks list, never a block.

2. Grips: the same ones, in the same places

Today

Each loose object has its own grips. Drag one and nothing else knows.

New

The same grips, where your hands expect them. Drag a pit grip and its joined pipe ends follow. Drag a label grip and only the label moves.

3. Drawing a pipe from one pit to another

1 · Type CD (or press Pipe). Hover a pit.

The pit under the cursor is outlined so you know it will be the start.

2 · Click anywhere on the pit.

The pipe starts exactly on the pit wall, in line with where you clicked.

3 · Move toward the next pit.

The pipe follows the cursor at full width. Size, length and where it will join show beside it.

4 · Click the second pit. Done.

Band, walls, label, arrow and both level texts appear at the standard distances. Levels read "not set" until you set them.

7. My honest thoughts on the whole thing

What I think is right

  • Connected, not mergedyour instinct about "sticky" is correct
  • Ordinary objects on the sheetthe only safe answer to the no-plugin problem
  • The engineer designsthe plugin shows, checks and carries
  • Small first releaseone real house or units job, start to issue

Where the risk is

  • Keeping joins alive through every AutoCAD commandmust be proven
  • An outsider edits the drawing, then sends it backmust be proven
  • Scope creeping back to 145 commandsone tool at a time, each approved
  • Breaking muscle memorykeep names, symbols and grips

What I would do next

  • 1You correct this draft until it is the plugin you mean
  • 2Research: prove the joins and the no-plugin promise on small trials
  • 3Settle the office standards list (about 15 rulings)
  • 4Design Pit and Pipe first, with you, in full
