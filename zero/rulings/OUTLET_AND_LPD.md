# Current rulings: Outlet and legal point of discharge

Current rulings as of 18 Nov 2026, 1:20 pm (Melbourne), from decisions D-001 to D-225. If this page and DECISIONS.md disagree, DECISIONS.md wins and this page is stale.

## What is decided now

1. **The council pit or kerb outlet that the new system connects into is a proper outlet a run can reach;** other existing drains stay hand-drawn in the first release (D-190).
2. **An Outlet tool is added to the first release** (THE_PLAN section 3 change note, citing D-190).
3. **The legal point of discharge label is its own small tool,** not part of the Outlet tool (D-222).
4. **The council connection invert is normally fixed on a tight site** (D-018), and the critical value, for example a high LPD invert, governs how levels are designed (D-017).
5. **The existing council pit connected to takes a row in the pit schedule** (D-110).
6. **P1 is at the outlet;** each system on a site is numbered from its own outlet, street system first (D-075, D-183).
7. **A loose run says it is not yet connected to an outlet;** head, orifice, storage and flows run only on a run that reaches the outlet (D-176).
8. **Today's practice:** the office applies to council for the discharge point; design often starts on an assumed discharge point or flow (D-030).
9. **An assumed connection invert prints "APPROX. IL 4.95" with one standing note** until the council letter confirms it (D-177).
