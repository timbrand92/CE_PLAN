## 1. Job kinds

### Common path seen on every job (so it is not repeated below)

1. **What arrives:** a fee proposal first, on "docs for quote" (town planning drawings, sometimes a sustainability report and soil report). Then the architect's CAD files. The council's legal point of discharge letter usually arrives from the architect or builder, not from the council to the office; on some jobs it is one to three years old when design starts. The planning permit or notice of decision, endorsed plans, landscape plan, arborist report, survey and "pit information" arrive as separate drops, often after the first issue.
2. **Set-up:** a "CVL set up" print of the architect's and surveyor's plans on the office sheet, dated one to five days after the CAD files.
3. **Order of issues:** set-up, mark-up, P1 (preliminary), sometimes P2, Rev A "for council approval", then Rev B, C ... as the council asks, then the same drawing re-stamped "construction issue".
4. **What goes to council:** the sheet, a computations PDF (two to four pages: required figures, storage list, orifice sum, then "WSUD"), the discharge letter, and the council's form (declaration, checklist) where it has one.
5. **Who checks:** the title block carries drawn / checked / approved initials on each revision row; council letters say the council checks only against its discharge conditions and that the building surveyor approves the system. The files do not show a separate office check sheet being filled in (a blank design / drafting / submission checklist workbook exists for two councils).

### a. Single house, no detention (CIV-01, Kingston)

1. **Arrives:** council discharge letter typed "single unit development", with three options (kerb by gravity or pump; existing council pit in the nature strip; on-site infiltration subject to soil) and NO permitted discharge figure. Underground services plans were ordered by the office. A "roof catchment area to water tank" sketch was made in the mark-up folder.
2. **Decisions:** no figure in the letter means no detention sum. Choose the option (council pit at the front). Tank size and roof area to tank come from the planning / sustainability side. Then pits, 100 pipes at 1 in 100, inverts back from the connection.
3. **Calculations:** none found in the folder (no computations PDF on disk). Not shown in the files whether pipe capacity was checked.
4. **Mark-up:** two dated mark-up folders (engineer's mark-up, then drafting) one day apart; contents are a drawing file and the roof-area sketch. Not viewed as pictures.
5. **On the sheet:** connection note "connect to council pit at approx IL 1.14 to council satisfaction, builder to confirm"; the existing council drains labelled with "depth to be confirmed"; a 3,000 litre underground reuse tank with its installation diagram; the council's standard notes; a note about the 1 in 100 year storm (overland flow). No detention criteria box.
6. **Dates:** discharge letter 20 Jun 2023; services plans 5 Jul; mark-ups 6 to 7 Jul; P1 11 Jul; Rev A 30 Nov 2023. What changed between P1 and Rev A: not compared.

5. **Dates:** letter 23 Aug 2023; Rev B for council submission 20 Jan 2025. Earlier issues not read.

### c. Two units, detention in tanks only (CIV-03, Kingston)

1. **Arrives, in order:** quote documents 24 Apr 2025 (town planning set, sustainability report, soil report); CADs 29 Apr; final CADs 12 May; on 13 May the discharge letter (dated 4 Apr, issued to another firm), the planning notice of decision and a "pit information" sheet. Endorsed plans arrived 25 Jun, AFTER the council issue.
2. **Inputs from the letter and permit:** permitted discharge 6 L/s (both the letter and permit condition say it); discharge to the kerb or to the council pipe at the front; kerb connections 1 m clear of the crossing splay; council standard drawings for kerb and pipe connections.
3. **Calculation 1, Boyd's (Kingston copy):** site 650.3; impervious 375.6; pervious 274.7; coefficient (375.6 x 0.9 + 274.7 x 0.35) / 650.3 = 0.65; effective area 420.45. 1 in 10 rainfall 98.6 / 73.6 / 60.1 / 51.5 / 45.5 / 40.9 mm/h for 5 to 30 minutes. At 5 minutes: inflow 11.52 l/s, volume in 3,455 litres, storage 3,455 x (1 - 6.00 / 11.52) = 1,656 litres. Largest of the column: 1.66 cubic metres.
4. **Calculation 2, storage:** two tanks at 1.00 each = 2.00, more than 1.66.
5. **Calculation 3, orifice:** flow = 0.6 x orifice area x square root of (2 x 9.81 x head), head = plate height less half the orifice diameter. 69 mm orifice, plate height 380: head 0.3455, flow 5.84 l/s, under 6.00.
6. **Mark-up:** on this job the mark-up is typed red notes over the first draft print, one day before issue: every pipe's size, class and grade ("150 dia UPVC SN8 1 in 100", "1 in 23"), tank notes, the council pipe size, the connection note with its invert, lid classes and pipe sizes on the orifice pit detail, tank orifice 30 mm.
7. **On the sheet:** criteria box (6.00 l/s, top water level 5.20, orifice 69, head 380, required 1.66, each tank 1.00, total 2.00); two roof-to-tank areas of 164; two-row pit schedule; orifice pit detail with this job's levels; council notes.
8. **Dates:** 21 days from quote documents to council issue; 2 working days from the last documents to council issue (13 to 15 May 2025). P1 and Rev A carry the same date.

### h and i. Soakage, with a raingarden and a STORM report (CIV-08, Frankston, four units)

1. **Arrives:** discharge letter from Dec 2021: "soak on site", with water sensitive design to the Melbourne Water infiltration chapter. A sustainability report and tree report with the quote documents (Feb 2023). CADs and landscape plan 27 Jul 2023. A permeability report was commissioned: falling-head tests in hand-augered holes on 10 Aug 2023, giving 259 mm/h at one hole and 124 mm/h at another, sand fill over sand over sandy clay, no groundwater seen.
2. **Decisions:** split the site into three catchments, each to its own soakage pit of plastic modules at the low points; each roof to a 3,000 litre tank first; driveway to a raingarden; silt pit before each soakage pit.
3. **Soakage sum (per pit, per storm length from 5 minutes to 7 days):** volume in = rainfall x effective catchment x minutes / 60. Volume out during the storm = (base area + half the perimeter x depth) x soil rate x moderating factor x time. Storage needed = in less out. Storage provided = width x length x depth x 0.95 (void share). The sheet prints OK or Inadequate for every storm length. Emptying time = stored volume / outflow rate.
4. **Worked example, pit 1 final:** catchment 647 (387.3 impervious, 259.7 pervious), effective 426.5; soil 259 mm/h; moderating factor 0.5 (sandy soil); modules 6 wide x 11 long, double height: 2.448 x 7.535 x 0.88 deep = 16.2 cubic metres gross; 1 in 100 rainfall; worst storm 90 minutes needs 15.37 against 15.42 provided (1.003: only just OK); empties in 4.4 hours.
5. **How it changed with the council:** Rev A (Oct 2023) was sized for the 1 in 10 year storm with moderating factor 1: pit 1 was 6.9 cubic metres. Council feedback (Oct 2023 and Feb 2024) asked, among other things, to "double check the computations for the soakage pits" and to arrange site testing. Rev B (Jan 2024) went to the 1 in 100 year storm; Rev C (Apr 2024) also applied the 0.5 factor on the sandy pits: pit 1 became 16.2, pit 2 9.8, pit 3 7.4. The pits on the sheet went to 60, 40 and 30 double modules.
6. **STORM report (2 Oct 2023):** each dwelling's roof (125 to 140) to a 3,000 litre tank; 34 of driveway to a raingarden of 1.0 square metre; 136 of driveway untreated; rating at least 100%. The final sheet labels a 3.5 square metre raingarden: the sheet and the report differ.
7. **On the sheet:** each soakage pit titled with its module count and grid, wrapped in geotextile, a typical section per pit, silt pit inverts, the raingarden section with 500 filter media layer, transition layer and overflow pipe with grated cap, tank details, the sewer main labelled with offset and depth.
8. **Rules used for soakage (from the checklist workbook and guidebook):** see section 3.
9. **Dates:** CADs 27 Jul 2023; set-up 31 Jul; soil test 10 Aug; P1 29 Aug; P2 15 Sep; sums and STORM 2 Oct; Rev A to council 3 Oct 2023; Rev B 25 Jan 2024; Rev C 23 Apr 2024. Nine months and three submissions.

## 2. Master list of inputs and outputs

### Inputs, by where they come from

1. **Council discharge letter / planning permit:** discharge point and option (kerb, council pit or pipe, easement drain, new outfall, soak on site, existing internal system); permitted discharge when given; storage when given; method and settings when given (coefficients before and after, time of concentration, travel time, storm years for discharge and for storage, rainfall zone); gravity only or pump allowed; approximate size, depth and offset of the council pit or pipe ("verify on site"); connection standard drawing numbers; splay clearance; treatment (STORM 100%) and reuse conditions; long section needed or not; fee and how to lodge; planning permit number and date; endorsed plans.
2. **Council by email (some councils):** permitted discharge and storage in reply to site and impervious areas; time settings.
3. **Survey:** title boundary lengths and bearings, spot levels and contours, kerb and channel levels, existing pits with cover and invert levels, benchmark, existing crossovers, trees, easements, neighbours' buildings; extra levels for an outfall route.
4. **Underground service plans and site investigation:** sewer main size, offset and depth, sewer ties, gas, water, telecom, power; pit photos and pipe depths; footing probes of neighbours.
5. **Architect / builder:** site and floor plans as CAD, floor levels, garage and driveway levels, roof plan and downpipe positions, tank positions and sizes, paving areas, basement levels and ramp, landscape plan, crossover positions and widths.
6. **Arborist:** tree protection zones and structural root zones.
7. **Soil report / tester:** soil profile, groundwater seen or not, saturated hydraulic conductivity per test hole (soakage); soil class for trench angle of repose.
8. **Sustainability / STORM report:** roof area to each tank, tank litres, reuse (toilets, laundry, irrigation), bedrooms or occupants, raingarden area and type, areas left untreated, rating.
9. **Bureau of Meteorology:** rainfall table for the site's coordinates (5 minute 1 in 5; 1 in 10 for 5 to 30 minutes; 1 in 10 and 1 in 100 at 2 hours for pumps; full table to 7 days for soakage; yearly and monthly rainfall for reuse).
10. **Product suppliers:** detention pipe outside and inside diameters; tank model, footprint and height; soakage module size (408 x 685, 450 single or 880 double high), void share; pump duty; modular tank volume; litter baskets and treatment devices; trench grate and lid classes.
11. **Engineer's choice:** which areas count as impervious; catchment split; pit positions, sizes and numbers; pipe sizes, grades, materials and classes; inverts and cover levels; top water level; plate (baffle) height; orifice size; which pipes and pits are detention; tank detention / reuse split; pump rate, well shape and size, rising main size and length; soakage pit size and position; moderating factor; overland flow path.

### Outputs that land on the drawing

1. **Plan:** pipes with size, material, class and grade; flow arrows; pits with number, cover level and each invert; downpipes; inspection openings; charged lines, risers and droppers; tanks with litres split; detention pipe runs with metres of detention; trench grates; agricultural drains; soakage pits with module grid; raingarden with area; pump station and rising main; connection note with approximate invert; finished and existing levels; overland flow arrows; roof-to-tank hatch and areas; clearances (splay, sewer, boundary).
2. **Boxes:** pit schedule (size, each inlet from / dia / invert, outlet dia / invert, cover level, depth to outlet, lid and remarks); detention criteria (permitted discharge, head, orifice, top water level, volume required, each storage by name, total); pump specification (rate, static rise, rising main, wet well volume); site areas where a council asks.
3. **Details carrying job numbers:** orifice pit (cover, top water level, baffle height, orifice, inverts, chamber sizes, lid classes); tank detail (litres, split, tank orifice, footprint); pump station section (seven named levels); soakage pit sections; raingarden section; long section rows; service crossing section.
4. **Sent beside the drawing:** computations PDF, STORM report, discharge letter, council declaration or checklist, sometimes pump specifications and the permeability report.

## 3. Rules and limits used, with source

1. **Minimum pipe grade 1 in 100** for gravity pipes: guidebook, sheet general notes. Detention pipes flatter: guidebook says some councils allow 1 in 200 for 225 and larger; the design checklist says 225 at 1 in 200, 300 at 1 in 250, larger at 1 in 300. Seen on jobs: 225 at 1 in 150, 1 in 190 and 1 in 200.
2. **Minimum underground pipe 100 dia:** guidebook. One council's comments: pipes between pits minimum 150, outfall minimum 300 class 2 concrete. Another: outfall minimum 225 class 2 concrete.
3. **Pipe under a garage: 150 UPVC SN8 with an inspection opening each side:** guidebook checklists, design checklist.
4. **Pipe cover:** standard's table (seen as a picture): plastics 100 for a single dwelling without traffic, 300 for other than single dwellings without pavement, 450 under traffic without pavement, 100 below reinforced pavement, 75 below light pavement. Guidebook thumb rule: 300 over detention pipe. Design checklist: minimum 200. One council: 250 everywhere. These four disagree.
5. **Detention pipe cover check:** cover = ground level less invert less (inside diameter + wall), from the maker's diameters (225: 259 outside, 226 inside); minimum pit depth = that + 300: pipe cover workbook.
6. **Pit size by depth:** guidebook table (350 x 350 to 450; 450 x 450 to 600; 600 x 600 to 900; 600 x 900 to 1,200 with step irons; 900 x 900 deeper). The detention workbook's own list differs (600 x 900, 900 x 900 and 1,200 x 900 all to 1.2). Step irons: over 1,200 on the sheet note, over 1,000 in the design checklist, over 900 in one council's comments.
7. **Orifice pit:** two chambers of at least 600 x 600, 100 clear from lid to top of baffle, 100 walls and 150 base (guidebook); wall 150 per one council's comment; baffle 150 thick if deeper than 1 m (another council, council guidebook workbook); one council uses a single chamber with a plate. Stepping in the detention workbook: orifice invert 20 above outlet invert, inlet invert 20 above that; pit inlets 10 above outlets.
8. **Minimum orifice:** 65 (two councils), 90 (one council, with an orifice only needed above 12 l/s), 50 (one council): guidebook and council guidebook workbook.
9. **Orifice formula:** coefficient 0.6; head = plate height less half the diameter: orifice workbooks.
10. **Share of detention underground:** all (one council, tanks not counted); at least 60% (one council; the council guidebook workbook says tanks at most 40%); at least 50% (several); "good practice 50%" elsewhere. Tanks: one council counts 500 litres of every 2,000 with no orifice, at least 1,000 above 2,500 litres with an orifice, and at least 50 square metres of roof to each tank; another counts a third of a reuse-connected tank; another a "dispensation" of 0.25 x storage x (roof connected / impervious area).
11. **Top water level** below every pit and grate level on the site (guidebook, checklist); at least 300 below habitable floors; ponding no deeper than 150 (council guidebook workbook, three councils).
12. **No detention pipes under dwellings or garages** (rear discharge under decking excepted): guidebook. Pipes under dwellings sleeved in some inner councils.
13. **Clearances:** 1 m from the crossover splay (council letters, checklists); 1 m from sewer ties (guidebook); new council pits 1 m from the crossover (two councils); no pits, pipes or paving in easements without consent (council comments); connect above the top third of the council drain (one council); stay out of structural root zones, tree protection zones only by supervised hand or hydro digging; do not undermine neighbours' footings (angle of repose detail: 45 degrees stiff clay to 30 degrees loose sand).
14. **Charged lines:** at least 300 from gutter sole to tank inlet; 135 degree bends with inspection openings, no 90s; tank slab level nominated (guidebook). Charged system workbook: 1 in 20 year 5 minute rain on the roof area; friction by the Hazen-Williams formula (roughness 150); fitting losses with a total factor of 4.5; the head left must pass more than the design flow.
15. **Pumps:** standard section 8 as copied in the office workbook: duplicate pumps; storage pumped in 30 minutes plus wet well at least the 1 in 10, 2 hour runoff; wet well at least 1% of catchment area and never under 3 cubic metres; high alarm no higher than 100 above the inlet invert; inlet invert at least 100 above top water level; ladder if deeper than 1.2 m. Office practice: pump rate = permitted discharge; basements with habitable risk for 1 in 100; well impervious, no groundwater; one council "discourages" pumps and wants prior approval; one requires gravity at the kerb.
16. **Soakage:** soil rate at least 100 mm/h; groundwater information; overflow for the 1 in 100; modules 1 m from footings, 2 m from boundaries, 1.5 m from tree zones where possible; silt trap with mesh before the modules; at the low point, not in a blocked-off area: design checklist and guidebook. Moderating factor 0.5 for sand, 1.0 for sandy clay (job sums, from the Melbourne Water procedure). Void share 0.95.
17. **Storm events and coefficients:** impervious 0.9; pervious 0.3 in the guidebook, pump and soakage workbooks, 0.35 in the Boyd's masters and some council settings; pre-development fixed at 0.4 by two councils, 0.35 by two others. Discharge storm 1 in 5 (most), 1 in 2 (one council), storage 1 in 10 (most), 1 in 20 (industrial council; sites sloping to the rear at another). Time of concentration 5 minutes for lots under 1,000 (Boyd's); 10 and 5 in OSD4W unless the council says otherwise (one said 7).
18. **STORM rating of 100%** for two dwellings or more at several councils; an offset payment where it cannot be reached; "usually no STORM report for two dwellings" at one council.
19. **Lids:** load class by use (A foot traffic, B driveways, C car parks, D warehouses and docks): guidebook picture; seen on jobs as class B and C on houses and units, class D on industrial.
20. **Driveway pavement** at least 125 thick with a centre invert and 1 in 30 crossfall where one council asks.
21. **Long section** horizontal 1:250, vertical 1:25 (one council); levels to the national datum.

## 5. Where the office's own documents disagree, or carry errors

1. **Pervious coefficient:** 0.3 (guidebook, pump, soakage) against 0.35 (Boyd's masters and job sums).
2. **Orifice constant:** the older orifice sheet writes "2.6 x area x root head" with gravity 9.8 (0.6 x root 19.6 is 2.66); the newer sheet uses 9.81 properly. A second older copy saved under one council's name uses coefficient 0.8 and 3.54, and still holds an earlier job's address and a 45 l/s example.
3. **"Head" on the drawing is not the head in the sum:** the criteria box prints the plate height (380) as head; the sum uses plate height less half the orifice (345.5). The Stage 1 note that checked this job used 0.38 and got 5.99 l/s; the job's own sum gives 5.84.
4. **Pit size labels against the numbers typed:** in three jobs' storage tables a pit labelled 600 x 600 is worked as 0.6 x 0.9, one labelled 450 x 450 as 0.6 x 0.6, one labelled 900 x 900 as 0.6 x 0.9 and one labelled 900 x 600 as 0.9 x 0.9. The volumes follow the typed numbers, not the labels.
5. **Detention workbook depth check:** the copied look-up on rows P3 to P5 points at the wrong cell (already noted in Stage 1); its tank line says "minimum storage for each unit 2,000 litres" with no source; its example leaves one pipe with minus 50 cover and no warning is carried to the total.
6. **Pump workbook versions:** one version sets top working level 100 below the inlet and takes static rise from it; another takes static rise from the inlet level itself. Bottom level is 400 below pump stop in one tab and 300 in another. Page 2 headers still carry other jobs' numbers; the workbooks hold earlier jobs' addresses in their cells. A "small pumps" tab lets the wet well be typed below the standard's 3 cubic metres (1.5 to 3 by a rule of thumb written beside it).
7. **Council guidebook workbook:** several council tabs are copies of another council's tab with the title or the notes not changed (one tab is headed with the wrong council; three carry another council's pump paragraph and standard-drawing note; one gives another council's email). It also says Boyd's for a council the guidebook lists as OSD4W only, and CIV-04 shows the cost of that: a Boyd's design was redone in OSD4W.
8. **Guidebook table 1** runs two council names together and lists one council under both "council supplies" and "OSD4W".
9. **Step iron depth, pipe cover and pit depth limits** each have three or four different values across the guidebook, the checklist, the detention workbook and the sheet notes (section 3).
10. **Job-level mismatches seen:** street number transposed in a computations header; sheet discharge and orifice not matching the latest sums (CIV-11); raingarden area on the sheet not matching the STORM report (CIV-08); council pipe size on the sheet (225) not matching the discharge letter's words (375) on CIV-03 (a pit information sheet in the folder may explain it; it is a picture and was not read).
11. **Template folders:** every job's civil folder is seeded with about 15 blank or stale sum files (Boyd's in four versions, pump in three), so file names cannot tell which sums a job really used.
