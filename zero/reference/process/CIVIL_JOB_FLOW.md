# How a civil job runs today: quote to construction issue

The steps of a civil (stormwater and pavement) job as the office files and the owner's answers show them. Re-filed from the evidence on Mon 16 Nov 2026. Read from files and interview answers; where they differ both are recorded. Jobs are coded; no client, address, job number or staff name is recorded.

## How to add to this file

1. Add a new numbered item at the END of the section it belongs to (never renumber), or a new section at the end of the file.
2. For a new job's dates, add one item to section 8 with its code (CIV-nn), its job kinds (K-numbers) and its dates in order.
3. If the files and the owner's words differ, record both under "Conflict:" and do not choose unless a D-number settles it.
4. End every section with an "Evidence:" line.

## 1. The steps in order

1. **Fee proposal.** Made on "docs for quote": town planning drawings, sometimes a sustainability report and soil report.
2. **Files arrive.** The architect's CAD files; the feature and level survey. Must have before starting (guidebook): the legal point of discharge location, the architect's plans and CAD files, the feature survey with site levels.
3. **Find the requirement.** Job kind (single house, units, commercial, industrial), which council (and Melbourne Water), and the site: slope, discharge point, existing and proposed conditions, council assets, other services, flood levels, neighbours' footings, trees to keep. The council's discharge document and the planning permit say whether detention is needed.
4. **Set-up.** A "CVL set up" print of the architect's and surveyor's plans on the office sheet, dated one to five days after the CAD files. The cleaned background drawing is made and attached (see `drafting\SHEET_SETUP.md` section 8).
5. **Computations.** Two numbers come first: the permitted site discharge and the on-site detention volume. Three ways, chosen by council: the council gives them; Boyd's in an office spreadsheet; OSD4W. The council's accepted method is confirmed first because the two methods can give different answers.
6. **Schematic design / mark-up.** Pits, detention pipes and tanks are laid out by trial and error on the A1 plans, usually near the discharge point at the low end of the site, working round slope, tree zones, neighbours' footings, sewer ties, paving, landscaping and the architect's intent.
7. **Water tanks and water quality.** Tank reuse volume from the STORM report, sustainability assessment or permit; how much of a tank may count as detention is set by each council.
8. **Drafting.** The design is marked up on an A1 sheet, then drafted; or, on small jobs, done straight in the drawing by one person (D-025).
9. **Check.** The engineer checks thoroughly after the first draft, and against the council's checklist.
10. **Preliminary issue (P1, sometimes P2).**
11. **Council issue (Rev A "for council approval").** With the computations PDF, the discharge letter and the council's forms.
12. **Council rounds (Rev B, C ...)** as the council asks.
13. **Approval,** then the same drawing re-stamped "construction issue".
14. **After construction issue:** further issues when clashes or "as built mark-ups" arrive.
15. **Making the cleaned background (detail of step 4):** Start from the office background template; paste the architect's site plan, ground floor and upper floor each at its 50,000 station; explode everything; move objects onto the office A- layers by kind; delete the architect's dimensions, room names, notes, tags and title block; keep the surveyor's text; add office content (boundary text, bench mark box, tree zones, existing drain). See E-056 (STUDY_D section 1.3 item 3)

Evidence: STUDY_B section 1 "Common path"; GUIDEBOOK_NOTES "The job in the guidebook's order"; INTERVIEW items 2, 8zg; DECISIONS D-025; STUDY_D section 1.3.

## 2. What arrives, and when

1. **First:** quote documents (town planning set, sustainability report, soil report).
2. **Then:** the architect's CAD files; sometimes "final CADs" about two weeks later (CIV-03).
3. **The council's legal point of discharge letter:** usually arrives from the architect or builder, not from the council to the office; on some jobs it is one to three years old when design starts, or issued to another firm.
4. **Conflict:** the owner's answer is that the office applies to the council for the discharge point (interview item 5; D-030), and that council discharge advice and permit conditions were NOT ticked as landing on the desk before design starts (interview item 2). The files show the letter usually arriving from the architect or builder. On CIV-11 the letter came two days after the office applied. Both recorded; not settled. When a fresh letter is asked for: Unknown (Q-009).
5. **Separate later drops, often after the first issue:** planning permit or notice of decision, endorsed plans, landscape plan, arborist report, survey, "pit information", underground services plans (ordered by the office on CIV-01), permeability report (commissioned on CIV-08), extra survey levels, footing probe, service scan, flood level certificate (CIV-05).
6. **From the council by email (some councils):** permitted discharge and storage, 3 to 4 days after the office sends site and impervious areas; 19 days after P1 on CIV-09.
7. **Unusual:** on CIV-11 a set of civil drawings and computations by an outside designer arrived, and the first computations lodged were that designer's file unchanged.

Evidence: STUDY_B section 1 "Common path" item 1, section 1a item 1, 1c item 1, 1e item 1, 1f item 1, 1h item 1, 1k item 1; INTERVIEW items 2 and 5; DECISIONS D-030.

## 3. What commonly arrives late, and what design starts on

1. **Design often starts on assumptions** that are updated when extra information arrives (the owner's words; D-030).
2. **Which assumptions most often change:** discharge point or flow; levels; tanks and water quality. (Not ticked: building layout or areas.)
3. **How an assumption is shown or tracked today:** no set way. On the sheet it is written as a note: "approx" invert "to council satisfaction, builder to confirm"; "depth to be confirmed"; the council notes say the invert at the discharge point is assumed; council pit and pipe sizes "to be verified on site".
4. **Seen arriving after an issue:** endorsed plans after the council issue (CIV-03); the council's figures after P1 (CIV-09); an amended discharge letter in Feb 2025 replacing the 2022 letter the design had begun on (CIV-05); a planning amendment after approval (CIV-06).
5. **What most often causes rework (the owner):** architect changes plans; council sends it back; drawing does not match.

## 4. Who does what

1. **Split of work:** about half and half: small house jobs by one person; units jobs go through a mark-up and a drafter (interview item 8i; D-030). Design and drafting meet either way, per job (D-025).
2. **Office time per typical job:** under half a day (up to about 4 hours), files in to drawings issued (the owner).
3. **Who measures site and impervious areas:** a mix, job by job: architect's figures, off the CAD, engineer or drafter (D-020).
4. **The mark-up, three forms seen:**
   1. Guidebook example: blue pen over a print of the survey and architect's plan: pits drawn and hatched, detention pipes drawn with a note such as "2 x 450 Stormpro, 9.5 m, for detention", pipe notes with size and grade, handwritten inverts and cover levels at each pit, "to match existing levels", crossover notes.
   2. CIV-03: typed red notes over the first draft print, one day before issue: every pipe's size, class and grade, tank notes, the council pipe size, the connection note with its invert, lid classes and pipe sizes on the orifice pit detail, tank orifice 30 mm.
   3. CIV-10: pen over the Rev A print: slab and surface levels at about 30 points, schedule figures struck out and rewritten, the static rise written into the pump box. So a mark-up at this stage is mostly levels.
5. **What a mark-up must carry before drafting starts:** Unknown (Q-007).
6. **Who checks:** drawn / checked / approved initials on each revision row. A blank design / drafting / submission checklist workbook exists for two councils; the files do not show it being filled in. The owner's list of what gets checked before issue: levels and falls; plan matches the numbers; against council's rules; drafting look-over. Who does it and when: Unknown (Q-008).
7. **Who confirms the method (Boyd's or OSD4W) with the council:** Unknown (Q-006).
8. **How the drafter works:** copy-and-fill from the parked kit in the template (see `drafting\SHEET_SETUP.md` section 1).

## 5. The issue sequence

1. **Set-up** (a print, not an issue).
2. **Mark-up.**
3. **P1** preliminary issue; sometimes **P2**.
4. **Rev A** "for council approval".
5. **Rev B, C, D ...** as the council asks.
6. **Construction issue:** the same drawing re-stamped.
7. **Later revisions** after construction issue (services clash; as-built mark-ups).
8. **Variation:** preliminary, tender, construction with no council stage seen (CIV-12).
9. **Sheets in a typical set (the owner):** drainage layout plan; notes and details; computations. Pipe long sections only when the council asks.
10. **Stamp and revision row mechanics:** see `drafting\TITLE_BLOCK_AND_ISSUE.md`.
11. **Tender stage on a public job:** CIV-15 (council is the client): P1 to P3 (preliminary), T1 to T4 (tender, rows reading "TENDER ISSUE"), then A (construction). A cut and fill plan was first issued alone at tender stage (STUDY_D section 4.8 items 6 and 7)
12. **Staged jobs:** CIV-14 was issued through revision E as "CVL ... STAGE 1 & 2" with the stage 1 and stage 2 plans on separate sheets (STUDY_D section 4.7 item 1)

Evidence: STUDY_B section 1 "Common path" item 3, section 1l item 3; INTERVIEW items 8 and 8b; ELEMENT_LIST "Across three civil jobs" moving part 7; STUDY_D section 4.7, 4.8.

## 6. What goes to council

1. **The sheet(s).**
2. **A computations PDF** (two to four pages: required figures, storage list, orifice sum, then "WSUD").
3. **The discharge letter.**
4. **The council's form** (declaration, checklist) where it has one.
5. **Sometimes:** STORM report, pump specifications, the permeability report.
6. **How:** each council has its own way in (online portal or email), forms, fees and a list of things it commonly asks for: see `reference\councils\`.
7. **What the council checks:** council letters say the council checks only against its discharge conditions and that the building surveyor approves the system.
8. **What comes back:** a "no objection" letter (Glen Eira); an approval letter that is itself a list of construction conditions (Monash); two letters, one for storage and discharge and one for the outfall (Darebin); amendment letters with numbered lists.
9. **What went with a house with detention (CIV-02):** The sheet, the computations PDF (a Word document of pasted spreadsheet pictures with three typed lines) and a declaration form (STUDY_D section 5.2 item 4)
10. **What the council gives back by e-mail (CIV-09):** A separate e-mail reply ("PSD and SSR parameters") supplied the permissible site discharge (7.48 l/s) and storage required (5.35 m3); the e-mail itself was not opened (STUDY_D section 5.5 item 1)
11. **A plain house sends no sums (CIV-01):** Nothing was sent to council as sums; the folder holds only blank template workbooks, the discharge letter, before-you-dig replies, two scanned hand mark-ups and one small PDF (STUDY_D section 5.1 items 1 and 2)
