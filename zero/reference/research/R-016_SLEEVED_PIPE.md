# R-016 A pipe sleeved inside a bigger pipe: how the office shows it

**Written:** Fri 20 Nov 2026, 12:10 pm (clock read), by Tim's room. Raised by Tim in TR-022: "under a garage we normally sleeve a 100dia. in a 150 upvc how do we handle this, check for drawings like this". Feeds unknown U-111.

## The question

How does the office show and word a sleeved stormwater pipe, and what must the Pipe tool do so the size rule ("never smaller downstream", D-263, TR-022 to TR-024) does not mistake a sleeve for a flow pipe?

## What was found (each source named in words)

1. **Office council checklist for one bayside council (a Word document in the office's Council Checklists folder).** Two rows: "Min 150 DIA upvc SN8 under garage slab" and "Charged pipes under slabs to be sleeved inside 150 DIA upvc SN8 pipe". **Checked at source by Tim's room.**
2. **One issued stormwater plan (a PDF plot in the office's civil folder; a real job, so no name is recorded).** Pipe labels read: "100 DIA. UPVC SEWER CLASS PIPE SLEEVED INSIDE A 150 DIA. UPVC SEWER CLASS PIPE AT 1 IN 100 MIN." and "100 DIA. UPVC SEWER CLASS PIPE CHARGED TO WATER TANK SLEEVED INSIDE A 150 DIA. UPVC SEWER CLASS PIPE". A standing note on the same sheet: "NOTE ALL CHARGED PIPES UNDER SLABS TO BE SLEEVED INSIDE A 150 DIA. UPVC SN8 PIPE". The sleeved run is drawn as ONE pipe with the long label, not two pipes. **Read by a research helper only; not re-read by Tim's room.**
3. **Office Civil and Drainage Design Guidebook (PDF, version 1.3 by file name), the page on inner metropolitan councils.** "Any pipes running under the dwellings need to be sleeved." The checklist pages for five councils repeat: "All pipes under the garage to be 150 DIA UPVC SN8" (a size rule, no sleeve). **Read by the helper only.**
4. **Office legend on civil sheets.** Has "NEW STORMWATER PIPE SUSPENDED UNDER SLAB" and "NEW CHARGED PIPE TO WATER TANKS 100 DIA. (U.N.O.)". No legend line for a sleeve. **Read by the helper only.**
5. **No standard detail of a sleeve was found.** The office's drawings (DWG) could not be searched for text; that proves nothing either way.

## What it means

1. **Two different rules that look alike.** (a) A SIZE rule: under a garage slab the pipe itself is 150 SN8 (several councils). (b) A SLEEVE rule: a charged pipe under a slab, or any pipe under a dwelling in the inner councils, is a 100 inside a 150.
2. **The 100 carries the water.** The label leads with the 100. The size check must read the 100 and ignore the sleeve.
3. **One pipe on the drawing.** The sleeve is words on the label, not a second drawn pipe.
4. **The class wording differs** between the label (SEWER CLASS) and the note (SN8) on the one sheet seen. Tim's to rule.

## Recommendation (one)

A sleeve is a property of a pipe, not a second pipe: a tick "Sleeved inside" with the sleeve's size and class (opening at 150 UPVC, from the office list). Ticked, the label gains "SLEEVED INSIDE A 150 DIA. UPVC ... PIPE"; the pipe is still drawn once at its own size; every size check uses the inner pipe. Kept as data rows, so a council that wants sleeves under dwellings can switch the tick on by itself later.

## Cost

Small: one tick and two fields on the pipe, one piece of label wording. No new drawn part. Not checked: how wide the band is drawn (100 or 150) on the office's own drawing file.
