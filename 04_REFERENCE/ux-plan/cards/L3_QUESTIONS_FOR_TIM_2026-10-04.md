# Level 3 proposals: 8 questions for Tim (4 Oct 2026)

**From:** team build/04 (runs 13-14), out of `plan-L3-LEVELS.md`, `plan-L3-SOURCES.md`, `plan-L3-NETWORK.md` and
`plan-L3-DETENTION.md`. None of these is answered anywhere in `C:\CE_PLAN` (checked the four plans, the four cards and

**For the desk:** ask in this order (1-2 first: they hold up the levels proposal). Record Tim's words in quotes.

---

1. **How steep may CE make a pipe when it proposes levels?**
   - **Recommended:** 1 in 20 for stormwater gravity pipes.
   - **On a job:** a 30 m pipe between two fixed levels 2 m apart needs 1 in 15. CE proposes 1 in 20 and shows the
     extra 0.5 m as a drop at the lower pit, for the drafter to decide. If you say 1 in 10, CE would lay that pipe at
     1 in 15 with no drop.

2. **A level typed in the Pit window (not on a pipe end): may CE move it?**
   - **Recommended:** no. Keep it fixed, the same as a level typed in Edit Pipe.
   - **On a job:** you type P4's invert as 21.35 from a survey sketch. CE never moves it. If 21.35 makes the run
     impossible, CE says so and names P4. If you say "movable", CE may change it to 21.20 like any level it worked out.

3. **Downpipes from roof edges: how far apart?**
   - **Recommended:** one at every low roof corner and valley, and no gutter run longer than 12 m between downpipes.
   - **On a job:** a 30 m straight gutter gets 3 downpipes. If you say 15 m, it gets 2.

4. **Which architect symbols count as downpipes?**
   - **Recommended:** layers with "DP", "DOWNPIPE" or "RWDP" in the name, and blocks named like "DP..." or
     "...DOWNPIPE...". CE offers each one for you to tick and never takes one silently.
   - **On a job:** a block "DP100" on layer "A-PLUMB-DP" is offered as a downpipe. A circle on "A-COLUMN" is not.

5. **Where do the proposals live?**
   - **Recommended:** one "Level 3 proposals" place in Project Review, with levels, downpipes, detention and layout
     side by side.
   - **On a job:** you open Project Review > Proposals and see "Levels: 6 pits" beside "Downpipes and sources: 14
     proposed". You do not hunt through four commands.

6. **When CE offers 2-3 network layouts, what should differ between them?**
   - **Recommended:** A "fewest pits", B "shortest total pipe", C "your usual set-off from the building".
   - **On a job:** on a 4-lot site, A has 9 pits and 82 m of pipe, B has 12 pits and 64 m, C follows your usual 1.0 m
     set-off from the house.

7. **How much must CE learn before it offers a layout at all?**
   - **Recommended:** at least 5 networks the drafter has drawn themselves, with learning switched on. Until then CE
     says "Not enough has been learned" and offers nothing (your 26 Sep ruling "Say so, offer nothing").
   - **On a job:** a new starter sees "Not enough learned yet" until they have drawn five networks. If you say 3, they
     see options after three.

8. **How close may a storage pipe, tank or pit come to a footing or a service before CE calls it a clash?**
   - **Recommended:** you give the distances; CE keeps them on the Settings page. CE recognises footings and services
     by layer or block names on a Settings list, confirmed on each job.
   - **On a job:** with a 0.5 m footing distance, a storage pipe 0.4 m from footing F3 shows "Clash: footing F3,
     0.4 m". Easements (the 1 m council-drain band) and tree zones already have your rules and need nothing new.

**Also found while building (not a question, for the desk to know):** most real lots already need the largest
standard storage pipe (DN600): a 600 m² lot with 300 m² of hard surface needs 15 m³, which is 53 m of DN600. So the
detention options will usually be "storage pipe" and "tank", not three pipes. A third option for those lots (for
example two shorter parallel runs) comes later, with the combination options (UX-L3-23).
