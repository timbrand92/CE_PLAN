## 1. The sheet set

1. **Typical two-storey house (A3, 1:100, 9 to 15 sheets):** general notes (2), ground floor footing plan with footing schedule and soil note, footing details (1 to 3), first floor framing plan, framing details and member schedule (1 to 5), roof framing plan with roof schedule and tie-down details, bracing details. Some jobs add articulation joints, remedial details or roof drainage. (Agrees with the two sets I read.)
2. **Small alteration:** 5 to 6 sheets.
3. **Warehouse (A1, plotted to A3 at half scale):** notes, slab and footing plan, mezzanine, roof, elevations, precast panels, details.
4. **Basement house (A1, about 14 sheets):** retention plan, elevations and sections, ground and first floor slab plans with separate extra-reinforcement plans, roof framing.
5. **How a set grows:** three hand-marked schematic plans, then preliminary P1 to P4, then revisions A, B, C. Sheets get renumbered along the way, so a sheet is known by what it is, not its number.

## 2. Elements on the plans

1. **Edge beam (EB):** solid outside face, dashed inside face, one mark.
2. **Internal beam (IB), thickening (TH), strip (SF), pad (PF):** dashed outlines on one shared layer; only the mark tells them apart.
3. **Other footing marks seen:** bored pier (BP), screw pile (SP), block wall (BW), capping beam (CB), soldier pile wall (SPW), band beam (BB), concrete column (RC).
4. **Beams:** solid line at the framing level, a separate "beam lower" line below it. Marks: FB, LB, LRB, RB, RL, L, GL (garage lintel), TB, TP, with CONT. or CANT. added.
5. **Joist, rafter and truss zones (FJ1, R1, RT):** a coloured cross line gives the span direction and a long arrow gives the extent. Truss area by hatch, with skylights cut out.
6. **Steel columns (C1 to C6, SC stub, BC bracing column):** symbol plus mark; "(O)" means from above.
7. **Stud packs:** DS and TS for double and triple studs.
8. **Load-bearing walls:** internal ones get a hatch; external walls are load-bearing without one. A fill shows the first floor outline over.
9. **Bracing:** strap, ply, and a fabricated steel bracing truss on some jobs.
10. **Label wording:** a mark only; the size sits in the schedule. Dwelling number in front of marks on dual occupancies.

## 3. How things relate

1. **Mark to schedule to detail:** every plan mark needs one schedule row and a detail for its junction.
2. **Load path:** roof trusses or rafters, to roof lintels and beams, to studs or columns, to floor beams, to columns, to thickenings, pads, strips or edge beams, to the founding soil.
3. **Beam ends:** a beam stops 100 short of its support; a beam drawn through a column is continuous or cantilevers over it. Touching lines prove nothing; levels and details decide.
4. **Between sheets:** the architect's plan is placed several times (ground over, ground under, first over, roof) 50,000 apart. "Under" copies show walls dashed. Columns above are dropped onto the plan below as "over" symbols.
5. **Must agree:** mark, schedule row, detail, calculation page, levels, drawing register, revision and certificate.

## 4. Where the numbers come from

1. **Office spreadsheets:** a beam design workbook (one page per member, with reactions), a footings workbook (pads, bearing, piers, screw piles), a slab design workbook, a suspended footing beam workbook and a loading sheet.
2. **Software named in the notes:** SpaceGass (continuous beams, frames, wind), RAPT and Inducta (suspended slabs), Wallap (pile walls).
3. **Soil report:** site class, founding depth, bearing.
4. **The engineer decides:** load paths, member marks, sizes, thickening positions, reinforcement and bracing, by hand mark-up of schematic plans and check plots. Adopted sizes can be larger than the calculated minimum.
5. **By others:** roof trusses and their bracing, open-web joists, precast, post-tensioning, screw piles.
6. **The drafter decides:** label placement, schedule order, which typical details stay. Span tables are not mentioned in the notes.
7. **Gap recorded:** several jobs have strip, edge or internal footing sizes with no calculation found.

## 5. From the architect

1. **Inputs:** plans, sections, elevations (levels, cladding, wet areas, stairs, voids, box gutters), plus survey and soil report.
2. **Set-up:** a cleaned copy is attached under the structure, clipped for each plan, faded. The original is never altered.
3. **Placement:** structure follows wall centrelines, sometimes column centres, rarely grids.

## 6. Standard details

1. **Footing family:** raft (edge beam by cladding type, internal beam, thickening, steps, mesh lap, re-entrant bars, penetrations, shower set-down), waffle, suspended raft, timber sub-floor, base plates.
2. **Framing family:** timber and steel beam-to-beam and beam-to-column, tie-downs, joist and rafter package, stub column, column over beam, fabricated lintels, bracing truss.
3. **Bracing family:** single, two and multi-storey packages.
4. **Others:** basement and retention, boundary protection, suspended slabs, block and retaining walls, alterations, stairs, pools, industrial.
5. **Varies by job:** cladding, soil depth, member sizes, step height, column type, plates and bolts. Full sets go on preliminary issues and are trimmed for the final.
6. **Status:** the library is described as donor material with duplicates and old values, not an approved master.

## 7. Job kinds

1. **Raft house:** raft, strips and pads poured as one; thickenings under point loads.
2. **Waffle:** ribs and pods, often on screw piles.
3. **Strip and stumps:** alterations with existing footings to be verified on site.
4. **Alterations:** existing, new and demolished shown separately.
5. **Townhouses:** marks prefixed by dwelling.
6. **Warehouses:** slab, pads, portal steel, purlins, precast panels, mezzanine.
7. **Basements:** soldier piles, capping beams, temporary props, staged sequence, transfer slabs.
8. **Pools:** usually by others, with nearby footings deepened.

## 8. Mistakes the notes recorded on real jobs

1. **Marks and headings:** a lintel mark with no schedule row; a roof beam under "Roof Lintels"; a member in the wrong schedule.
2. **Sheets:** duplicated details, a blank "refer sheet S__", missing truss notes, two different sets issued under one revision.
3. **Calculations:** carrying another job's number, dated after issue, or disagreeing with the schedule.
4. **Depths and levels:** footing depths disagreeing between plan, schedule and soil report; levels differing between architect and civil.
5. **Paperwork:** certificates describing the wrong building; file names not matching title blocks.
